## Key Takeaway

1. **Superlatives need "the" (Unit 90)**: the highest, the most expensive, the best, the worst. Don't drop "the" before a superlative adjective.
2. **Spelling: double consonants (Unit 87/90)**: one-syllable adjectives ending in consonant-vowel-consonant double the final consonant: thin → thinner → thinnest, big → bigger → biggest. Don't write "thinest."
3. **Superlative + "in" not "of" (Unit 90)**: use "in" for groups: "the tallest in the class," "the best in the world." Not "of."
4. **Comparative word order (Unit 88)**: quantifiers go before the comparative: "two years older," "much bigger," "a bit cheaper." Not "older two years."
5. **Third-person singular -s (Unit 86)**: "she runs," not "she run." Always add -s to the verb after he/she/it.
6. **too + adjective, not too + verb (Unit 92)**: "too hot to drink" (too + adj + to + verb). "Too drink" is not a valid form.
7. **Adjective vs adverb — same meaning (Unit 86)**: "She speaks English well" and "Her English is good" mean the same thing. "Well" is the adverb after the verb; "good" is the adjective after "is."
