## Key Takeaway

- Keep the verb and its object together: `bought a backpack`, `finished his report`.
- Put place before time: `I met her at the station yesterday`.
- Put `always`, `usually`, `often`, and `never` before a main verb: `I usually go`.
- Put these adverbs after `be`: `She is always late`.
- Put `yet` at the end of negative sentences and questions: `He hasn't arrived yet`.
- Put `still` before the main verb or after `be`: `I still work here`; `I am still waiting`.
- With `give`, `send`, `lend`, and similar verbs, use either `I gave Ben the keys` or `I gave the keys to Ben`.
- A singular countable noun usually needs a determiner: `my cousin`, `the canteen`, `an email`.
- In grammar labels, distinguish a direct object, an indirect object, and a complement.
