## Key Takeaway

1. **Present perfect vs past simple (Unit 15)**: use present perfect for past actions with a **present result** ("I've lost my keys" = I can't find them now). Use past simple for actions at a **specific past time** ("I saw that film yesterday" — not "I have seen yesterday"). Rule: if you say **when** it happened (yesterday, last week, at 8 o'clock), use **past simple**, not present perfect.
2. **gone vs been (Unit 17)**: **has gone** = went and is **still there** (she's in London now, not here). **has been** = went and **came back** (she is here now, has the experience). "Has been to Paris three times" = visited and returned three times.
3. **just, already, yet (Unit 16)**: **just** = happened a moment ago. **Already** = happened before now (positive sentences). **Yet** = until now (negative sentences and questions). Word order: **yet** goes at the **end** of the sentence ("I haven't seen it yet") or after "haven't" ("I haven't yet seen it"), not before "haven't."
4. **for vs since (Unit 18)**: **for** + a **duration** (for three years, for two months). **since** + a **starting point** (since 2019, since Monday, since I was born). Think: "for how long?" vs "since when?"
5. **How long + present perfect (Unit 18)**: "How long **have you lived** here?" — present perfect for duration continuing to now. "How long **do you live** here?" is wrong — present simple is for habits, not duration.
6. **Question marks**: a question always ends with **?** — "Have you been to Japan?" not "."
