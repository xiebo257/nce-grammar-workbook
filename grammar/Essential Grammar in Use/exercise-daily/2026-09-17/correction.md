## Key Takeaway

1. **Get out OF a car/taxi (Unit 114)**: when you name the vehicle, use the full form "get **out of** the taxi" — the preposition **of** is required before the object. Without an object, "I got out" alone is fine. This was the error in 21.5.
2. **Pronoun placement rule (Unit 115)**: a pronoun object (**it/them**) must go **between** the verb and the particle: "put **it** back," "turn **it** off," "take **them** back." A **noun** can go either position: "put back the phone" or "put the phone back." This was the error in 21.14 — the prompt asked for a pronoun but the learner used a noun.
3. **Get on/off vs. get in/out (Unit 114)**: for public transport you step onto (bus, train, tram, plane) → **get on / get off**. For vehicles you sit inside (car, taxi) → **get in / get out of**. The learner correctly distinguished these in 21.10.
4. **Go off vs. turn off (Unit 114/115)**: **go off** = something happens by itself (an alarm goes off, food goes off); **turn off** = someone stops a device. The learner correctly identified this contrast in 21.12.
5. **Separable phrasal verbs with nouns (Unit 115)**: a noun object can go either side of the particle — "turned the fan off" or "turned off the fan" — with no meaning difference. Both are correct.
