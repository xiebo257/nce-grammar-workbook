## Key Takeaway

1. **Three future forms, three uses (Units 25-28)**:
   - **Present continuous** (I'm eating) = fixed arrangement (time/place already agreed with others)
   - **going to** (I'm going to eat) = intention or evidence-based prediction (decided earlier, or seeing signs)
   - **will** (I'll eat) = spontaneous decision, promise, or prediction without evidence (decided at the moment of speaking)
2. **Present simple for timetables (Unit 25)**: trains, flights, and scheduled events use the **present simple**: "The train **leaves** at 9.30," not "is leaving."
3. **going to vs will for predictions (Unit 26 vs 27)**: when you can **see evidence now** (dark clouds, someone stumbling), use **going to** ("It's going to rain"). When you're making a **general prediction** without immediate evidence, use **will** ("I think it will be a good year").
4. **Spontaneous decision = will (Unit 28)**: if you just decided to help now → "I'll help you." If you decided yesterday → "I'm going to help you." The key question: did you decide **now** or **earlier**?
5. **Look at + object**: the verb "look" needs the preposition **at** before an object: "Look **at** those clouds," not "Look those clouds."

## One-line Summary

Strict score 9/15 (5 notes + 1 error); main focus: going to is for intentions decided earlier (not "will"), present continuous is for fixed arrangements (not "going to"), and present simple is for timetables — match the form to how and when the decision was made.
