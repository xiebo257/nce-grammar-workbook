## Key Takeaway

1. **Match tense and subject:** `rooms are cleaned` (routine); `three people were injured yesterday` (past event). The plural subject needs `are` or `were`, not `is`.
2. **Use the right passive sequence:** present continuous `is/are being + past participle` describes an ongoing action; present perfect `has/have been + past participle` describes a completed action relevant now.
3. **Keep the original meaning when correcting:** `The shirts have been ironed` preserves completion. `Are being ironed` would describe a different, ongoing action.
4. **Translate the result as well as the action:** `These trees have already been cut down` expresses `已经`; `were cut down` alone does not make that result explicit.
5. **Place `just` naturally:** `have just been taken` puts the time adverb after the first auxiliary.
