## Key Takeaway

1. **Past continuous agreement:** use **I/he/she/it was + -ing** and **we/you/they were + -ing**. “Our neighbours **were having** dinner,” not “was having.”
2. **Complete past continuous forms:** do not omit the auxiliary. Write “Ava **was studying**” and “her brother **was playing**,” not just “studying” and “playing.”
3. **Past simple negatives:** use **didn't + base verb**. Write “didn't leave,” not “wasn't” or “didn't left.”
4. **Prepositions and time expressions:** write a letter **to** someone, wait **for** the bus, walk **into** the kitchen, and say **at three yesterday afternoon** rather than “at three at noon.”
5. **Two past-action patterns:** simultaneous actions use past continuous in both clauses (“While Ava was studying, her brother was playing”); an interruption uses past continuous plus past simple (“While Ava was studying, the lights went out”).
