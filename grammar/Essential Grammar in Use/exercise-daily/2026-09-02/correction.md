## Key Takeaway

1. **this/these = near the speaker; that/those = far (Unit 74)**: "Is **this** your phone?" (in your hand) / "**That's** a great idea!" (referring back to what someone said). On the phone, identify yourself with "This is…"
2. **some vs any (Unit 75)**: **some** in affirmative statements ("I bought some bananas"); **any** in questions and negatives ("Did you buy any?" / "I didn't buy any"). Note: "some" is also fine in offers/requests ("Do you want some bread?").
3. **one vs ones (Unit 76)**: **one** replaces a singular countable noun ("a clean one"), **ones** replaces a plural noun ("the black ones"). Never say "a clean" or "the black" alone after an adjective.
4. **no vs not … any (Unit 77)**: "There are **no** shops" (positive verb + no = zero) = "There **aren't any** shops" (negative verb + any). Both mean zero; don't use "some" when the meaning is negative.
5. **someone / anyone / no one (Unit 77)**: someone → affirmative ("There's someone"); anyone → questions & negatives ("Is there anyone?"); no one → affirmative verb but zero meaning ("There's no one" = not anyone).
6. **Double negatives are wrong (Unit 77)**: "We don't have no money" → "We have no money" or "We don't have any money." Never two negatives together.
7. **a vs an is about sound (Unit 65)**: "an old car" (vowel sound), "a new car" (consonant sound). This rule still applies inside longer sentences — check every singular countable noun.
