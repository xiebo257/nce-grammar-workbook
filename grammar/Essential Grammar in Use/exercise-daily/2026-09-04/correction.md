## Key Takeaway

1. **mustn't vs don't need to (Unit 31)**: **mustn't** = prohibition — you are not allowed to do it (You mustn't smoke here = smoking is banned). **Don't need to** = not necessary — you can, but you don't have to (You don't need to bring a gift = it's optional). The clue "It's not necessary" signals **don't need to**, not **mustn't**.
2. **Modals take no "to" (Unit 30)**: can, could, must, should, might — all followed by the **base form** without "to." ✅ "He can drive" (not "can to drive"), ✅ "You mustn't park" (not "mustn't to park").
3. **Could you...? for polite requests (Unit 30)**: "Could you pass the salt?" is more polite than "Can you pass the salt?" The book teaches "Could you...?" as the standard polite request form.
4. **Would you like vs Do you like (Unit 34)**: **Do you like** = asking about general preference (Do you like coffee? = do you enjoy coffee in general). **Would you like** = offering one now (Would you like a coffee? = do you want one right now).
5. **Too much vs more**: "太多糖" = **too much sugar** (excessive amount). "More sugar" = additional sugar (not necessarily excessive). Use "too much" for "太多."
6. **Plural countable nouns**: "no buses" (plural), not "no bus" (singular). Buses are countable — use the plural form when talking about zero quantity.
