## Key Takeaway

1. **there is/are = existence; it is = distance/time/weather/fact (Units 37, 39)**: "There is a cat in the garden" (something exists) vs "It is raining / It's cold / It's ten o'clock / It's a long way to the station." Never say "there is cold" or "it is a cat in the garden."
2. **Uncountable nouns take singular there was (Unit 38)**: "There **wasn't any milk**" (milk = uncountable → singular was), not "weren't." Water, money, bread, rice → singular.
3. **there is/are, NOT there have (Unit 37)**: English uses "there is/are" for existence, not "have." "There are a lot of problems" ✅; "There have a lot of problems" ✗.
4. **Lists agree with the nearest noun (Unit 37)**: "There's a book and two pens on the table" — with a singular noun first, "there is/there's" is standard. ("There are a book and two pens" is also accepted, but the nearest-noun pattern is most natural.)
5. **Watch the tense and the vocabulary**: the prompt set the tense (past) and the words (书 = book, not pen). Read the Chinese carefully before translating.
6. **Time and distance both use it (Unit 39)**: "It's ten o'clock" (time), "It's ten kilometres from here to the airport" (distance) — both it, not there.
