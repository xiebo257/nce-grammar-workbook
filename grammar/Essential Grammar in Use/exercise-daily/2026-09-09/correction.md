## Key Takeaway

1. **Third-person singular -s / -es (Units 2–5)**: in the present simple, he/she/it (and uncountable/singular subjects like "water") take **-s** or **-es**: boil → **boils**, watch → **watches** (verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -o add -es). "Water boil" ✗ and "He watch" ✗ are the most common slips.
2. **-ing spelling rule — short C+V+C verbs double the consonant (Unit 23)**: swim → **swimming**, run → running, sit → sitting. "swiming" (single m) is wrong. Contrast with verbs ending in silent -e (write → writing) and most others (play → playing).
3. **Present simple vs. present continuous**: present simple = habits and permanent facts ("I usually work 9 to 5", "Where do you live?"); present continuous = happening now ("Right now I am working", "Where are you going?"). The "Look, …" signal marks the continuous.
4. **It is (not It has) for weather (Unit 6)**: "It's cold today" — describing weather/state uses **be**, not **have**.
5. **don't vs. doesn't (Unit 5)**: with I/you/we/they → **don't**; with he/she/it → **doesn't**. "He don't" → "He doesn't".
6. **have got for possession (Units 8–9)**: "I've got a car" = I have a car. Common in spoken British English.
