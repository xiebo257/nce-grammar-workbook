## Key Takeaway

1. **Pronoun placement rule (Unit 115)**: when a separable phrasal verb (put on, take off, pick up, turn off) takes a **pronoun object** (it, them, him, her), the pronoun **must** go between the verb and the particle: ✅ "pick it up," ❌ "pick up it." A **noun** object can go either position: "pick up the book" or "pick the book up."
2. **Intransitive phrasal verbs (Unit 114)**: verbs like "go in," "walk in," "get off," "take off" do not take a direct object. To mention a destination, use a preposition: "walked **into** the house," not "walked in the house."
3. **Get on vs get off (Unit 114)**: for buses, trains, and planes, use **get on** (enter) and **get off** (leave). For cars, use **get in** and **get out (of)**.
4. **Go out (Unit 114)**: a fire or light **goes out** (stops burning) on its own — not "was stopped." "Turn off" is for devices you control (lights, heating), not for natural fire.
5. **Separable phrasal verbs with nouns (Unit 115)**: "put on your jacket" and "put your jacket on" mean the **same thing** — the noun can go either position. There is no meaning difference.
