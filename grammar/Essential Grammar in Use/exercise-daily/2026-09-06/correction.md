## Key Takeaway

1. **too vs either (Unit 42)**: **too** = also (for positive sentences: "I'm hungry too"). **either** = also (for negative sentences: "I'm not hungry either"). Rule: positive → too; negative → either.
2. **So vs Neither (Unit 42)**: **So + aux + I** = agreeing with a positive ("I love chocolate" → "So do I"). **Neither + aux + I** = agreeing with a negative ("I don't like chocolate" → "Neither do I"). Never use "So don't I" — use "Neither do I."
3. **Matching the auxiliary to the verb (Unit 42)**: the auxiliary in "So/Neither" must match the verb tense of the original statement:
   - Present simple "love" → **do**: "So do I" (not "So am I" — "am" is only for "I am...")
   - Present continuous "am going" → **am**: "So am I"
   - Past simple "went" → **did**: "So did I"
   - Present perfect "have seen" → **have**: "So have I"
   - Modal "can" → **can**: "So can I" / "Neither can I"
4. **Auxiliary to avoid repetition (Unit 40)**: "I like coffee, but my wife **does**" (does = likes). "I didn't go, but my friends **did**" (did = went). Match the tense and person.
5. **Question tags (Unit 41)**: positive statement → negative tag ("It's cold, isn't it?"). Negative statement → positive tag ("It isn't cold, is it?").
6. **Modals take no "to" (Unit 43)**: can swim, could run, must go, should eat — never "can to swim."
