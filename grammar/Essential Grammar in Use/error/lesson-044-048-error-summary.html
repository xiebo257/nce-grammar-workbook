Rule: who vs what for subject questions

46.1 #6 "The book was written by XXXXX." Who wrote the book? Who was the book written by? active vs passive Grammatically correct but exercise keeps preposition "by" at the end — preserves passive structure.
46.2 #3 Picture: a man going to a restaurant. "Which restaurant _____?" are you going to is he going to pronoun Picture shows a man → he (3rd person), not you. Match pronoun to picture subject.
46.3 #3 "Jack plays for a football team." → Which...? Which team is he play for? Which team does he play for? auxiliary Present simple Q = does + base verb, not is + base verb. Mixed continuous+simple forms incorrectly.

Rule: present simple question form
Present simple question = do/does + subject + base verb. Never mix "is" (continuous auxiliary) with a base verb. "is he play" → "does he play".

Rule: What vs Which vs Who
What = open-ended (infinite options). Which = limited choice (from a known set). Who = people (preferred over Which for people).

Lesson 48 — How long does it take...?

#QuestionYour answerCorrectIssue
48.2 #4 "Walk from your home to the nearest shop" It takes ten minutes to walk to the neartest shop. (personal — correct grammar) typo neartest → nearest.
48.3 #4 "Tom painted the bathroom." → How long did it take...? How long did it take Tom to...? How long did it take him to...? pronoun preferred Key prefers pronoun "him" (but "Tom" is grammatically correct).
48.3 #5 "They repaired the car." → How long did it take...? How long did it take they to repair the car? How long did it take them to repair the car? pronoun case they → them (object pronoun after "take"). Subject: I/he/she/we/they; Object: me/him/her/us/them.
48.4 #2 "left 10:00, arrived 10:20" It took us twenty mintues to walk to home. It took us 20 minutes to walk home. two issues "to home" → "home" (home = adverb, no "to"); also typo "mintues" → "minutes".
48.4 #5 "began a long time ago, got a job last week" It took Lisa a week to look for a job. It took Lisa a long time to find a job. two issues Wrong duration ("a week" vs "a long time") + wrong verb ("look for" = searching, "find" = result).
Rule: object pronouns after "take"
After "take", use object pronouns: me / him / her / us / them. Subject pronouns (I, he, she, we, they) are wrong here. "It took them to repair the car."
Rule: "home" is an adverb
home = adverb (no preposition needed). "walk home" (not "walk to home"). Same for: go home, come home, get home, arrive home.
Rule: look for vs find
look for = the process of searching (ongoing). find = the result (completed). "It took a long time to find a job" = the result was achieved after a long search.
