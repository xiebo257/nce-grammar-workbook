Rule: no preposition before "tomorrow + time of day"
"tomorrow night", "tomorrow afternoon", "tomorrow morning" — no "at", "in", or "on" before these phrases. They function as adverbials on their own.
Rule: 3rd person singular in present simple
he/she/it → add -s or -es: the course finishes, the bus leaves, he plays.

Lesson 26 — I'm going to...

am/is/are going to + infinitive for intentions and predictions based on evidence.

#QuestionYour answerCorrectIssue
26.1 #2 Picture: someone about to take a bath. "I" + input am going to have a batch. am going to have a bath. wrong word "batch" (a group) → "bath" (washing).
Rule: bath vs batch
bath = washing yourself (have a bath, take a bath). batch = a group of things processed together (a batch of cookies, a batch of files).

Lesson 27 — will/shall 1

will + infinitive for future facts, predictions. 'll = will, won't = will not.

#QuestionYour answerCorrectIssue
27.1 #2-7 "Complete with she was, she's or she'll be" — all 6 answers (verb only, no subject) she'll be / she was / she's / she was / she'll be systematic All 6 answers missing subject "she"; tense choices all correct.
Rule: always include the subject
In English, the subject is mandatory — even when the exercise frame provides context. Write "she'll be", not just "'ll be". The subject can never be dropped in standard English.

Rule: write the full option in "which is right" exercises
When choosing between two options, write the complete option, not just the verb. "I'll do" (not "I'll"), "I watch" (not "watch"), "is going to buy" (not "to buy").

Lesson 29 — might

might + bare infinitive for uncertain possibility ("maybe, but I'm not sure").

#QuestionYour answerCorrectIssue
29.1 #6 "(it's possible that Mark will not be here next week)" I might not be here next week. Mark might not be here next week. wrong subject Prompt is about Mark, not "I".
29.2 #3 "When will you see Kate again? I'm not sure." (item: see her on Monday) I might see him on Monday. I might see her on Monday. pronoun Kate is female → "her" not "him".
29.4 #2 "Write three things you might do tomorrow" I might cooking. I might cook. verb form Modal + bare infinitive (might cook), not -ing (might cooking).
29.4 #3 "Write three things you might do tomorrow" I might go to cenima. I might go to cinema. spelling cenima → cinema.
Rule: might + bare infinitive
might + verb (no "to", no -ing, no -s): might cook / might go / might not be. Same as all modal verbs.
Rule: match pronouns to names
Kate → she/her. Mark → he/him. Always check who the sentence is about before choosing a pronoun.
