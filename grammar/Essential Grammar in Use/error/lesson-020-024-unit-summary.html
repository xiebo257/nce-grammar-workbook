Unit 20 — I have done (present perfect) vs I did (past simple)

What it means

Present perfect (have/has + past participle) connects a past action to the present — it is still relevant now. Past simple describes an action that is completely finished and disconnected from now.

TenseFormWhenExample
present perfecthave/has + past participleno finished time — experience up to nowI have seen her a few times.
past simpleverb + -ed (regular) / irregular formfinished time (yesterday, last week, ago)I saw her yesterday.
When to use present perfect vs past simple — the "finished time?" test
Time markerTenseExample
yesterday, last week, two days agopast simpleI switched off the light before going out.
this morning (when the morning is over)past simpleI switched off the light before going out this morning.
ever, never, a few timespresent perfectI have seen her a few times.
"now" (signals a period is finished)past simpleI lived in Mexico for many years. (but I live in New York now)
"before that" (past period)past simpleShe worked there for two years.

Key: If there is a finished time word or the context shows the action is over → past simple. If there is no finished time and the experience connects to now → present perfect.

Why "now" signals past simple

"I live in New York now, but I lived in Mexico for many years." The word "now" establishes that the current situation is different from the past. The Mexico period is finished. Using "have lived" would imply you still live in Mexico, contradicting "now."

Similarly, "before that" refers to a period that ended before the current situation → past simple.

Correct examples
  • ✅ "I have seen her a few times, but I have never spoken to her." (experience, no finished time → present perfect)
  • ✅ "I switched off the light before going out this morning." (finished past context → past simple)
  • ✅ "She worked there for two years, but she didn't enjoy it." (past period → both verbs past simple)
Pitfall — "for" does not always mean present perfect

"For many years" can be used with past simple when the period is clearly finished:

  • ✅ "I lived in Mexico for many years." (past — now I live elsewhere)
  • ✅ "I have lived in Mexico for many years." (present perfect — I still live there)

The deciding factor is whether the period is still continuing, not the word "for."

Form rules
  • present perfect: have/has + past participle (have seen, has gone, have lived)
  • past simple: verb + -ed (regular) or irregular form (saw, went, lived)
  • After "Yes, " in a continuing clause, keep lowercase: "Yes, she went."

Unit 21 — is done / was done (passive 1)

What it means

The passive shifts focus from the doer to the action or result. Structure: be + past participle.

TenseSingularPluralExample
present passiveis doneare doneMany programmes are shown on TV.
past passivewas donewere doneThe bridge was built in 1885.
When to use present vs past passive
ContextTenseExample
general ongoing fact (no past time marker)present passiveMany American programmes are shown on British TV.
specific past eventpast passiveWhen was this bridge built?
personal fact (birthplace)past passiveMy mother was born in China.
Why passive shifts focus

The passive is used when the action or result is more important than who did it. "The bridge was built in 1885" focuses on the bridge, not the builders. "Many programmes are shown on British TV" focuses on the programmes, not who broadcasts them.

Subject-verb agreement in passive

SubjectCorrect be-formExample
singular (the bridge, this picture)is / wasThe bridge was built.
plural (films, programmes, houses)are / wereFilms are made here.
Correct examples
  • ✅ "A film studio is a place where films are made." (films = plural → are)
  • ✅ "It was given to me by a friend of mine." (give → was given)
  • ✅ "I wasn't woken up by the noise." (wake → woken, not woke)
Pitfalls
  • ❌ "films is made" → ✅ "films are made" (plural subject → are)
  • ❌ "wasn't woke up" → ✅ "wasn't woken up" (past participle of wake = woken, not woke)
  • ❌ "was found" (when verb box says "give") → ✅ "was given" (match the verb to the context, not to a random word)
Form rules
  • passive = be + past participle: is done / are done / was done / were done
  • Plural subject → are/were; singular subject → is/was
  • Use the correct past participle: wake → woken, build → built, give → given, show → shown

Unit 22 — is being done / has been done (passive 2)

What it means

Extending the passive to present continuous (happening now) and present perfect (completed, result visible now).

TenseFormWhenExample
present continuous passiveis/are being + donehappening right nowThe road is being repaired at the moment.
present perfect passivehas/have been + donecompleted, result nowThe car has been damaged.
present passiveis/are + donegeneral factMany programmes are shown on TV.
past passivewas/were + donespecific pastThese houses were built in the 1980s.
When to use which passive tense
Time marker / contextTenseExample
"at the moment" / right nowis/are being doneThe kitchen is being painted at the moment.
completed, result visible nowhas/have been doneThe car has been damaged.
general fact (no time marker)is/are doneEnglish is spoken here.
specific past (existing things)was/were doneWhen were these houses built?
Why existing things take past passive

"How old are these houses? When were they built?" — the houses already exist, so the building is a completed past action. You cannot say "when are they built" because the building is not happening now. The present tense "are built" would imply they are being built right now or are built repeatedly, which doesn't make sense.

Test: If the thing exists and you're asking about its origin → past passive (was/were done).

Correct examples
  • ✅ "The car has been damaged." (full sentence — items with no starter text need subject + verb)
  • ✅ "When were they built?" (existing houses → past passive)
Pitfalls
  • ❌ "are they built" (for existing houses) → ✅ "were they built" (completed past)
  • ❌ Missing subject when no starter text → ✅ Write a full sentence: "The car has been damaged."
Form rules
  • Present continuous passive: is/are being + past participle (is being done, are being built)
  • Present perfect passive: has/have been + past participle (has been damaged, have been repaired)
  • When no starter text is given, write a full sentence with the subject

Unit 23 — be / have / do in present and past tenses

What it means

Choosing the correct auxiliary verb (be, have, or do) depends on the verb form that follows the blank.

Verb form after blankAuxiliary familyPresentPastExample
bare verb (go, walk, eat)do-familydo / doesdidThey don't go out very often.
-ing form (going, walking)be-familyam / is / arewas / wereShe is going home.
past participle (gone, eaten)have-family (perfect)have / hashadI have finished.
past participle (done, seen)be-family (passive)is / arewas / wereIt is done.
When to use which auxiliary — the "verb form" test
  • Look at the verb form after the blank
  • Bare verb (go, walk, like) → do/does/did
  • -ing form (going, walking) → am/is/are/was/were
  • Past participle + perfect meaning → have/has/had
  • Past participle + passive meaning → is/are/was/were
Why "don't" not "aren't" for "go out"

"They don't go out very often." — the verb after the blank is go (bare infinitive). The do-family (do/does/did) is used with bare verbs for negatives and questions in simple tenses. Using "aren't" (be-family) would produce "aren't go out," which is grammatically broken — "be" never combines with a bare infinitive.

Habit/frequency expressions ("very often," "every day") use simple tense → do/does/did.

Correct examples
  • ✅ "They don't go out very often." (bare verb → do-family)
  • ✅ "She is working." (-ing → be-family)
  • ✅ "I have finished." (past participle → have-family)
  • ✅ "It was done." (past participle → be-family, passive)
Pitfall — be + bare verb is broken

❌ "They aren't go out very often." — wrong! "be" (aren't) never combines with a bare infinitive (go).

✅ "They don't go out very often." — correct! "do" (don't) combines with bare infinitives.

Rule: be = state/continuous (is going, was working). have = perfect (have gone). do = simple (don't go, does he know?).

Form rules
  • Bare verb → do/does/did (don't go, does she know, did they come)
  • -ing → am/is/are/was/were (is going, were walking)
  • Past participle (perfect) → have/has/had (have finished, has gone)
  • Past participle (passive) → is/are/was/were (is done, were built)

Unit 24 — Regular and irregular verbs

What it means

Every verb has three forms: base / past simple / past participle. Regular verbs add -ed for both past simple and past participle. Irregular verbs have unique forms that must be memorized.

When to use which form
ContextFormExample
finished past (with time marker)past simpleI lived in Mexico for many years.
after have/has (present perfect)past participleI've never met his wife.
after be (passive)past participleIt was given to me.
Why three forms matter

Different tenses require different verb forms. Using the wrong form changes the meaning or breaks the grammar:

  • "I met her yesterday." (past simple — specific past event)
  • "I've never met his wife." (past participle after have → present perfect)
  • "It was given to me." (past participle after be → past passive)

For irregular verbs, the past simple and past participle may differ (wake → woke → woken), so you must know both.

Correct examples
  • ✅ "I know Gary, but I've never met his wife." (past participle of meet = met)
  • ✅ "I wasn't woken up by the noise." (past participle of wake = woken)
  • ✅ "It was given to me by a friend." (past participle of give = given)
Pitfalls
  • ❌ "I've never known his wife" (when box verb is "meet") → ✅ "I've never met his wife" (match the verb to the box, not to a word already in the sentence)
  • ❌ "wasn't woke up" → ✅ "wasn't woken up" (past participle of wake = woken, not woke)
  • ❌ Confusing past simple with past participle for irregular verbs where they differ: wake → woke (past) vs woken (participle); write → wrote (past) vs written (participle)

Rule: Match the verb to the meaning and the box, not to a word already in the sentence. "Know" is in the sentence, but the exercise asks for "meet."

Form rules
  • Regular verbs: base + -ed for both past simple and past participle (live → lived → lived)
  • Irregular verbs: memorize all three forms (meet → met → met, wake → woke → woken)
  • After have/has → use past participle (have met, has gone)
  • After be (passive) → use past participle (was given, is shown)
  • For past simple → use the past simple form (lived, met, woke)
