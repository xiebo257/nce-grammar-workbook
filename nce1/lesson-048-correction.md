## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Singular: `a`/`an` + adj + `one` | `I don't want a small one. I want a big one.` | `one` replaces a singular countable noun. The article (`a`/`an`) goes before the adjective. Vowel sound → `an` (`an old one`). |
| Plural: `any`/`some` + adj + `ones` | `I don't want any red ones. I want some green ones.` | `ones` replaces a plural countable noun. `any` in negation, `some` in affirmation. Numbers can replace `some`: `two red ones`. |
| Demonstrative shift | `this one` → `that one` | Replace `this` with `that` when declining. |
| Full noun phrase alternative | `I don't want any coffee. I want a cup of tea.` | When the cue is a full noun phrase (a cup of tea, a cake, a glass), negate the original and state the alternative directly. |
| `I like X, but I don't like Y.` | `I like cheese, but I don't like butter.` | Split `X and Y` into like + X, don't like + Y, joined by `but`. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q4 — spelling `cofee` → `coffee`.** The word has two `f`s: c-o-f-f-e-e. A common misspelling — double-check double consonants in high-frequency words.
- **Q5 — missing article `an` before `old one`.** The pattern requires `a`/`an` + adjective + `one`: "I don't want **an** old one." The article is not optional — `old one` without an article is ungrammatical. **Vowel sound → `an`** (`an old one`, `an empty one`). The second sentence `I want a new one.` is correct (consonant sound → `a`).
