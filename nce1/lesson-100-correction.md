## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Reported present perfect | `He says that he's already got a licence.` | Change quoted `I have` to `he has` while keeping the completed-state meaning. |
| First-person viewpoint | `What do you say?` -> `I say that I feel cold.` | When the respondent is the quoted speaker, `I` can remain `I`. |
| Pronoun shift to third person | `My friends say that they've enjoyed themselves.` | A named third-person subject requires `they/them/their/themselves`, not `we/us/our/ourselves`. |
| Possessive viewpoint shift | `Your son` -> `my son`; `our city` -> `their city` | Adjust possessives to the reporting speaker's viewpoint. |
| Modals and `had better` | `She says that she must catch the train.` | `Can`, `must`, and `had better` do not need a tense change here; adjust the subject pronoun when necessary. |
| `be going to` | `They're going to have a holiday.` | Keep the auxiliary `be` after the subject and before `going to`. |
| Future `will be` | `It will be very hot.` | Keep `be` after `will` before an adjective such as `hot`. |
| Articles with singular count nouns | `an X-ray`, `a driver's licence` | Singular countable nouns need `a` or `an`. |
| Regional spelling | `licence` / `license` | `Licence` is the British noun spelling; `license` is the American noun spelling. |
