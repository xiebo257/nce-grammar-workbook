## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `a` + singular countable noun | `a cup`, `a pencil`, `a pen`, `a piece of paper` | Use `a` before a singular countable noun. **`piece` is countable** → "a piece of paper", not "some piece of paper". |
| `an` + singular countable (vowel sound) | `an orange`, `an apple` | Use `an` before a vowel sound. |
| `some` + uncountable / plural countable | `some water`, `some milk`, `some flowers`, `some chalk`, `some ink` | Use `some` with uncountable nouns (water, milk, ink) or plural countable nouns (flowers) in affirmative statements and offers. |
| `any` + uncountable / singular countable (negative/question) | `any paper`, `any pen`, `any pencil`, `any coffee` | Use `any` in negatives and questions with both uncountable and countable nouns. |
| `some` in offers | `Would you like some tea?` | `some` (not `any`) is standard in polite offers/requests, even though the form is a question. |
| Measure words | `a piece of paper`, `a slice of cake`, `a bar of chocolate`, `a bottle of ink`, `a cup of tea`, `a tin of tobacco`, `a loaf of bread`, `a pound of sugar`, `a glass of water`, `a pint of milk` | Each measure word pairs with specific nouns. Learn the collocations. |
| `a` + measure + `of` + uncountable | `a piece of paper`, `a bar of chocolate`, `a bottle of ink` | The pattern is `a + measure noun + of + uncountable noun`. The `a` goes before the measure noun, not the uncountable noun. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q23 — `some` vs `a` before `piece`.** `piece` is a singular countable noun — it takes `a`, not `some`. The pattern is `a + measure + of + uncountable`: "a **piece** of paper", "a **bar** of chocolate", "a **bottle** of ink". The `a` goes before the measure word, and `some` goes before the uncountable noun directly: "some ink". **Rule: `a` before singular countable measure nouns; `some` before uncountable or plural nouns.**
