## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `across` (from one side to the other) | `swimming across the river` | Used for crossing a surface/area — river, street, bridge. |
| `between` (in the middle of two) | `walking between two girls` | Used when someone/something is in the middle of **two** things or people. Not interchangeable with `across`. |
| `along` (following the length) | `running along the wall` | Movement parallel to the length of something. |
| `beside` (next to) | `sitting beside his grandmother` | Position immediately next to someone/something. |
| `under` (below) | `flying under the bridge` | Position directly below something. |
| `into` (entering) | `going into the park` | Movement from outside to inside. |
| `out of` (exiting) | `running out of the shop` | Movement from inside to outside. |
| `after` (chasing) | `running after our cat` | Movement behind/pursuing something. |
| `for` (waiting) | `waiting for a bus` | `wait for` = expect the arrival of. |
| Set 3 frame: verb + object + location | `She's cleaning her teeth. She's cleaning her teeth in the bathroom.` | First sentence: verb + object. Second sentence: repeat verb + object + add location. No period between the object and the location — they form one sentence. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q3 — `across` vs `between`.** "John's walking **between** two girls" means he is in the middle of them. `across` means crossing from one side to the other (e.g., "across the river") and doesn't work with "two girls." When the sentence describes someone in the middle of two people/objects, use `between`.
- **Q22 — `batchroom` → `bathroom`.** The word is **bath** + **room** = bathroom (a room with a bath). "Batchroom" is not an English word. Break compound nouns into their parts to spell them correctly: `bath` + `room`.
- **Q22 — misplaced period.** "She's cleaning her teeth. in the batchroom." has a period that breaks the location phrase into a sentence fragment. The location phrase "in the bathroom" must attach to the verb: "She's cleaning her teeth in the bathroom." — one sentence, no period between object and location.
