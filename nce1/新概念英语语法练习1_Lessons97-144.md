# NCE Grammar Practice 1 - Lessons 97-144

Source: `新概念英语语法练习 1.pdf`.  
This structured transcription records the final NCE1 lesson sequence, grammar focus, exercise directions, examples, and representative practice patterns. Original answer lines are condensed and blanks are represented by `___`.

## Lesson 97 - A small blue case
### I. Complete noun phrases and descriptions
Practice adjective order, colour, size, material/object vocabulary, articles, and `one/ones`.
### II. Ask and answer about objects
Use *Which one is ...?*, *What colour/size is ...?*, and possessive answers.

## Lesson 98 - Whose is it?
### I. Complete ownership questions and answers
Use *Whose is this/that?*, possessive `-'s`, possessive pronouns, and singular/plural forms.
### II. Rewrite with possessive pronouns
Replace repeated noun phrases with `mine`, `yours`, `his`, `hers`, `ours`, and `theirs`.

## Lesson 99 - Ow!
### I. Complete health and accident dialogues
Practice body parts, hurts, injuries, `must`, imperatives, and object pronouns.
### II. Give instructions and advice
Use *Don't ...*, *Be careful*, *Show me ...*, and short responses.

## Lesson 100 - He says that ...
### I. Change direct speech into reported statements
Practice `He/She says that ...` with present-tense statements, pronouns, and possessives.
### II. Complete reporting dialogues
Use `say/says`, `tell/tells`, and changes in person, place, and time references.

## Lesson 101 - A card from Jimmy
### I. Complete present-perfect sentences
Use `has/have`, past participles, letters/cards, places, and travel vocabulary.
### II. Rewrite direct messages as reported information
Use *He says that ...* and *He has ...* forms.

## Lesson 102 - He says he ...
### I. Change statements into reported speech
Use *He says he ...* / *She says she ...* with present simple, present continuous, and possession.
### II. Complete dialogues
Practice pronoun changes, contractions, and reporting verbs.

## Lesson 103 - The French test
### I. Complete school and test dialogues
Practice past simple, scores/results, `can/could`, and adjectives describing performance.
### II. Report what people say
Use introductory clauses such as *The teacher says that ...*.

## Lesson 104 - Too, very, enough
### I. Fill in the blanks with `too`, `very`, or `enough`
Practice adjective and adverb placement: *too small*, *very expensive*, *old enough*, *quickly enough*.
### II. Rewrite with the correct degree expression
Describe people, clothes, rooms, objects, and abilities.

## Lesson 105 - Full of mistakes
### I. Complete descriptions with quantity and degree words
Practice `full of`, `too many`, `too much`, `enough`, and countable/uncountable nouns.
### II. Correct statements and give advice
Use errors in writing, spelling, work, and school contexts.

## Lesson 106 - I want you to ...
### I. Make sentences using `want + object + to do`
Example pattern: *I want you to type this letter.*
### II. Change commands into `want` sentences
Use object pronouns and infinitives for household, school, and office actions.

## Lesson 107 - It's too small
### I. Complete comparisons of size and fit
Use `too`, `enough`, adjectives, clothing, and object descriptions.
### II. Give reasons and alternatives
Practice *It's too ...*, *It isn't ... enough*, and `one/ones`.

## Lesson 108 - How do they compare?
### I. Form comparative adjectives
Use short and long adjective comparative forms, including spelling changes.
### II. Ask and answer comparison questions
Use *Which is ...?*, *Is A ... than B?*, and complete comparative sentences.

## Lesson 109 - A good idea
### I. Complete suggestion and plan dialogues
Practice *Let's ...*, *Why don't we ...?*, `shall`, and `going to`.
### II. Use comparative forms in practical situations
Compare choices, places, prices, routes, and objects.

## Lesson 110 - How do they compare?
### I. Complete comparative sentences
Use irregular comparatives such as `better`, `worse`, `more`, and `less`.
### II. Rewrite using `than`
Compare people, activities, weather, prices, and qualities.

## Lesson 111 - The most expensive model
### I. Form superlative adjectives
Use `the -est` and `the most ...`, including irregular forms.
### II. Ask and answer about a group
Use *Which is the most/least ...?* and superlative descriptions.

## Lesson 112 - How do they compare?
### I. Review comparative and superlative forms
Choose the correct form for two-item and group comparisons.
### II. Complete dialogues
Use `than`, `the`, `in/of`, and adjective forms correctly.

## Lesson 113 - Small change
### I. Complete money and quantity dialogues
Practice change, prices, coins, notes, `some/any`, and `one/ones`.
### II. Ask for and give amounts
Use *How much ...?*, *How many ...?*, and shopping responses.

## Lesson 114 - I've got none
### I. Fill in the blanks with `some`, `any`, `no`, or `none`

### II. Rewrite avoiding repetition
Use *I've got none*, *I haven't got any*, and object pronouns.

## Lesson 115 - Knock, knock!
### I. Complete arrival and visitor dialogues
Practice *Who's there?*, *Come in*, requests, responses, and object pronouns.
### II. Report information and instructions
Use `say/tell`, present simple, and imperative forms.

## Lesson 116 - Every, no, any, some

### II. Change statements into questions and negatives
Preserve correct countability and singular/plural agreement.

## Lesson 117 - Tommy's breakfast
### I. Complete breakfast and routine descriptions
Practice meals, quantities, present simple, and habits.
### II. Ask questions about meals and preferences
Use *What does ... have?*, *Does ... like ...?*, and short answers.

## Lesson 118 - What were you doing?
### I. Make past-continuous questions and answers
Use *What were you doing?*, `was/were + -ing`, and time expressions.
### II. Contrast past continuous and past simple
Complete sentences about interrupted actions and events.

## Lesson 119 - A true story
### I. Complete a past narrative
Use past continuous and past simple in story order.
### II. Ask and answer about the narrative
Use *What happened?*, *What was/were ... doing?*, and time/place cues.

## Lesson 120 - It had already happened
### I. Complete past-perfect sentences
Use `had + past participle`, `already`, `before`, `after`, and sequence markers.
### II. Contrast past simple, past continuous, and past perfect
Rewrite events in chronological order.

## Lesson 121 - The man in a hat
### I. Join descriptions with relative clauses
Use `who`, `which`, `that`, and location phrases to identify people and things.
### II. Make identifying questions and answers
Use *Who is the man who ...?* and *Which is the ... that ...?*

## Lesson 122 - Who (whom), which, and what
### I. Fill in the correct relative/interrogative word

### II. Combine pairs of sentences
Make clear noun phrases and questions with relative clauses.

## Lesson 123 - A trip to Australia
### I. Complete travel and experience descriptions

### II. Ask and answer about a trip
Use *Have you ever ...?*, *When did ...?*, and past/perfect contrasts.

## Lesson 124 - Who (whom), which, and what
### I. Review relative clauses and question words
Select correct words for people, objects, and actions.
### II. Rewrite for clarity
Use object relative forms, prepositions, and complete questions.

## Lesson 125 - Tea for two
### I. Complete food and drink invitations
Practice offers, requests, quantities, preferences, and polite replies.
### II. Use `would like`, `would rather`, and `want`
Make affirmative, negative, and interrogative conversational forms.

## Lesson 126 - Have to and do not need to
### I. Complete sentences with `have to`, `don't have to`, and `needn't`

### II. Give practical advice
Use work, school, travel, and home situations.

## Lesson 127 - A famous actress
### I. Complete a biographical description

### II. Ask questions about a person

### II. Complete deduction dialogues

## Lesson 129 - Seventy miles an hour

## Lesson 130 - He can't have been ...

## Lesson 131 - Don't be so sure!
