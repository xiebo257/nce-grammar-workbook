## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Verb–object collocation | `air + room`, `sharpen + pencil`, `sweep + floor`, `dust + cupboard`, `empty + box` | Each verb has a natural object — match by meaning, not by word shape. |
| `shut` = close | `Shut your books.` / `Shut the door.` | `shut` and `close` are interchangeable for books, doors, windows. |
| Phrasal verbs | `turn on the light`, `turn off the tap`, `put on your tie`, `take off your shoes` | The particle (on/off) comes before the object. `turn on` = activate; `turn off` = deactivate; `put on` = wear; `take off` = remove. |
| `meet` + person | `Come and meet our new office assistant.` | `meet` introduces a person; the object must be a person, not a thing. |
| Imperative + `please` | `Sweep the floor, please.` | The cue `verb/object` expands to `[Verb] [object], please.` — capitalize the verb, add `please` at the end. |
| `What must I do?` frame | `What must I do? — Sweep the floor, please.` | The question `What must I do?` asks for an instruction; the answer is an imperative sentence. |
