## Usage table

| Pattern | Example from this lesson | Practical reminder |

| Possessive adjective for one female owner | `What's her name?` | Use `her` for something belonging to one female person. |
| Possessive adjective for a group | `What are their jobs?`; `our friends` | Use `their` for other people and `our` for the speaker's group. |
| Speaker/listener viewpoint | `What's your job?`; `my pen` | `Your` refers to the person addressed; `my` refers to the speaker. |
| Singular job question | `What's his job?` | Use `is` and singular `job` for one person. |
| Plural job question | `What are their jobs?` | Use `are` and plural `jobs` for two or more people. |
| Who-question agreement | `Who is this man?` / `Who are these women?` | Match `is` to one person and `are` to more than one. |
| Demonstratives and number | `this man` / `these two men`; `that girl` / `those people` | Keep singular `this/that` and plural `these/those` aligned with the noun. |
| Paired question structure | `Who are they? What are their jobs?` | Answer both parts: identify the people, then ask or state their occupations. |

## Key takeaway

- Match each possessive adjective to its owner: `my`, `your`, `his`, `her`, `our`, or `their`.
- Match number throughout a question: one person takes `is` and `job`; two or more take `are` and `jobs`.
- Use `Who is ...?` for one person and `Who are ...?` for a pair or group.
- In a paired prompt, keep both questions and use the plural form `their jobs` for groups.
- Contractions are fine, but write them with a standard apostrophe: `Who's` and `What's`.
