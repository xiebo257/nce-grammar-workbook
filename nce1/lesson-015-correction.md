## Usage table

| Pattern | Example from this lesson | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Regular plural `noun + s` | `girl -> girls` | Add `-s` to most countable nouns. |
| Plural after `-s` | `case -> cases` | Add `-es` when the spelling ends in `s`. |
| Noun ending in `-ie` | `tie -> ties` | Add `-s` to the existing `-ie` ending. |
| Plural short answer | `Are the cars clean? No, they aren't.` | Use `they` and `are` for plural things or people. |
| Singular `is` | `Is your girl friend fat or thin?` | Use `is` with one person or thing. |
| `we/you/they + are` | `We are Norwegian.` | Use `are` with `we`, `you`, and `they`. |
| `Here is/are` | `Here is my dress.` / `Here are our passports.` | Match `is` or `are` to the noun that follows. |
| Two blanks in one sentence | `My friend is ... His two brothers are ...` | Choose `be` separately for each subject. |

## Key takeaway

- Form regular plurals with `-s` and use the appropriate spelling pattern, such as `case -> cases` and `tie -> ties`.
- Use `they are` in short answers about plural nouns.
- Match `is` and `are` to the subject: singular nouns use `is`; `we`, `you`, `they`, and plural nouns use `are`.
- In `Here is/are ...`, agree with the noun after `here`.
