## Usage table

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `has/have already been + past participle` | present perfect passive (already done) | `It has already been cleaned.` |
| `will be + past participle` | future passive | `The job will be done soon.` |
| `Has anyone + past participle ... yet?` | present perfect question | `Has anyone caught the thief yet?` |
| `hasn't/haven't been + past participle yet, but will be + past participle + time` | not done yet, will be done at a specific time | `He hasn't been caught yet, but he will be caught this week.` |
| `plural possessive: days'` | apostrophe after the `s` for plural | `in two days' time` |

## Key takeaway

- **In Part III, the time frame from the prompt must appear in the answer**, not in the question, and not replaced with `soon`. Items 19-23 all used `soon` instead of the specific time (`this week`, `tomorrow`, `next week`, `this evening`).
- **`it` vs `he`**: a thief is a person, so use `he` (or `they`), not `it`.
- **Plural possessive**: `two days' time` — the apostrophe goes after the `s` because `days` is plural.
- **Don't drop content from cues**: item 14 dropped `green` from the cue `wall/paint/green/next week`.
