## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Adverb before action verb | `often rides`, `sometimes goes`, `always come`, `often ask` | Frequency adverbs (`always`, `often`, `sometimes`) go **before** the action verb. |
| Adverb after `be` verb | `It's sometimes mild`, `It's always windy`, `It isn't often windy` | With `be` verbs, the adverb goes **after** `be`: `is/are + adverb + adjective`. |
| `every day` at the end | `goes to the shops every day` | `every day` is a time expression, not an adverb — it goes at the end of the sentence. |
| Negation: `don't`/`doesn't` + adverb + base verb | `doesn't always speak`, `don't often buy` | After `don't`/`doesn't`, the verb reverts to **base form** (no `-s`): `speaks` → `speak`, `washes` → `wash`, `does` → `do`. The adverb stays between `don't`/`doesn't` and the base verb. |
| Negation of `be`: `isn't`/`aren't` + adverb + adjective | `isn't always windy`, `isn't often pleasant` | `be` negation: `isn't`/`aren't` + adverb + adjective. No base-form change needed. |
| `Which...?` → `[Subject] likes [cue] best.` | `He likes Chinese best.` / `I like the Brazilian team best.` | Answer with the subject + `likes` + the full cue + `best`. Preserve the entire cue noun phrase. |
