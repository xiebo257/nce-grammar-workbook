## Usage table

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `I` → `he/she` | first-person speaker → third person | `I am drinking` → `he was drinking` |
| `we` → `they` | first-person plural → third-person plural | `We are going to` → `they were going to` |
| `you` → `him/her/them` | second-person listener → matching third person | `buy you an ice cream` → `buy them an ice cream` |
| `my` → `his/her` | possessive shift | `my son` → `her son` |
| `your` → `his/her` | possessive shift (listener's) | `your mother` → `his mother` |
| `mine/yours` → `his/hers` | possessive pronoun shift | `mine` → `hers`, `yours` → `his` |
| present → past (backshift) | tense backshift in reported speech | `is` → `was`, `has done` → `had done`, `did` → `had done` |

## Key takeaway

- When the speaker and listener are clear from context, shift all first-person pronouns (`I`, `we`, `my`, `our`, `mine`) to third person matching the speaker, and all second-person pronouns (`you`, `your`, `yours`) to third person matching the listener.
- Shift tenses one step back: present → past, present perfect → past perfect, simple past → past perfect.
- For sentences with multiple clauses (item 14: `You look ill. I am sure there is something wrong with you.`), apply pronoun and tense shifts to every clause consistently.
