# NCE Grammar Practice 1 - Lessons 25-48

Source: `新概念英语语法练习 1.pdf`, printed pages 67 onward.  
This is a structured transcription of the lesson headings, instructions, examples, and representative prompt sets from the scanned workbook. Repetitive answer lines are condensed; blanks are retained as `___`.

## Lesson 25 - Mrs. Smith's kitchen

### I. Fill in the blanks with `a`, `an`, or `the`

1. There is ___ new table in our kitchen. It's in ___ middle of ___ kitchen.
2. There is ___ watch in ___ cupboard in your room.
3. These glasses are big. ___ ones on ___ table are dirty; ___ ones in ___ cupboard are clean.
4. Give me ___ knife, please.
   Which one?
   ___ sharp one, not ___ blunt one!
5. Is there ___ pen on my desk?
   Yes, there's ___ pen and there's ___ pencil. ___ pencil is a red one.
6. There is ___ refrigerator in Mrs. Smith's kitchen. ___ refrigerator is ___ white one.
7. What's ___ matter, John?
   I'm hot. Give me ___ ice cream, please.
8. Who is that man?
   He's ___ American tourist.
9. Here are two coats, ___ black one and ___ blue one. Which one is your coat? ___ black one or ___ blue one?
   My coat is ___ blue one.
10. Come and meet Molly. She's ___ keyboard operator.

### II. Fill in the blanks with `in` or `on`

1. There is a picture ___ the wall.
2. There is water ___ the bottle.
3. There is a small table ___ the room.
4. My pens are ___ the desk.
5. There's a big box ___ the floor.
6. The tickets are ___ her handbag.
7. Where are my shoes?
   They're ___ the cupboard.
8. Jane's small bottles are ___ her dressing table.
9. Look at the new carpet ___ my room!
10. Is there a cooker ___ her new kitchen?
    Yes, there is.
11. The table is ___ the middle of the room, and the cups and plates are ___ the table.
12. There's the new cooker. And the new refrigerator is ___ the right.

### III. Answer the questions using the cues according to the example
Example: *Is there a spoon in the cup? (on the plate/dirty)* -> *No, there isn't a spoon in the cup. There is one on the plate. It's dirty.*

1. Is there a spoon in the cup? (on the plate/dirty)
   *No, there isn't a spoon in the cup. There is one on the plate. It's dirty.*
2. Is there a tin on the table? (in the refrigerator/big)
3. Is there a bottle on the cupboard? (in the cupboard/full)
4. Is there a table in the middle of the room? (in the kitchen/brown)
5. Is there a knife on the plate? (on the shelf/blunt)
6. Is there an umbrella on the floor? (on the chair/red)
7. Is there a tie on the bed? (on the dressing table/orange)
8. Is there an electric cooker on the right of the cupboard? (on the left of the cupboard/new)
9. Is there a magazine on the desk? (on the bed/new)
10. Is there a window on the left of the door? (on the right of the door/open)
11. Is there a knife on the plate? (on the tin/green)
12. Is there a box on the shelf? (on the refrigerator/empty)

## Lesson 26 - Where is it?

### I. Fill in the blanks with `a`, `an`, or `the`

1. Is there ___ book on ___ table?
   Yes, there is. There is ___ big red book.
   Give me ___ book, please.
   Here you are.
2. There are some glasses here. ___ glasses on ___ shelf are clean. ___ glasses on ___ table are dirty.
3. Look at John's new car. What make is it?
   It's ___ Fiat.
4. My new friend is ___ American student.
5. Is this your pen? Is it ___ blue one?
   Yes, it's ___ blue one. It's my pen. Thank you.
6. There is ___ orange bottle in the refrigerator. ___ bottle is full.
7. On ___ right there is ___ television and on ___ left there is ___ desk.
8. There's ___ cigarette shop and it's open.
9. There's ___ electric cooker in our kitchen. It's new and it's white.
10. My father is ___ engineer. I am ___ son of ___ engineer.

### II. Ask and answer using the cues according to the example
Example: `my key/on the table` -> *Where is my key? It's on the table.*

1. `my key/on the table`
   *Where is my key? It's on the table.*
2. `your pencil/on the book`
3. `my coat/on the bed`
4. `your fork/on my plate`
5. `the refrigerator/in the kitchen`
6. `your pen/in my schoolbag`
7. `my watch/on the dressing table`
8. `the newspaper/on the television`
9. `the stereo/on the cupboard`
10. `the television/in the middle of the room`

## Lesson 27 - Mrs. Smith's living room

### I. Fill in the blanks with `in`, `on`, or `near`
Describe a classroom, teacher, students, a table, flowers, windows, a clock, and a picture using locations such as in the school/classroom/room, on the table/wall, and near the table/open window.

### II. Rewrite the sentences according to the examples
Change singular `There is/There's a ...` sentences into plural `There are some ...` sentences, then change questions such as *Is there a chair in the kitchen?* into *Are there any chairs in the kitchen?*

### III. Ask and answer questions according to the example
Example: *Are there any sharp pencils on the desk?* -> *No, there aren't any sharp pencils on the desk. There are some blunt ones in my schoolbag.*

Use paired cues for clean/dirty glasses, big/small plates, red/yellow flowers, long/short spoons, new/old chairs, full/empty bottles, dirty shirts, hats, and leaves.

## Lesson 28 - Where are they?

### I. Fill in the blanks with question words
Use `Where`, `Whose`, `Who`, `What`, `Which`, `What colour`, or `What make`.

Prompts include tickets in the kitchen, Anna's pen, magazines, a Volvo, a black coat, Jane, a French nationality, a coat on the bed, trousers belonging to a brother, and an armchair near a window.

### II. Answer questions using the cues
Example: *Where are my cigarettes? (in the living room/on the table)* -> *They're in the living room. They're on the table.*

Practice plural locations for flowers, shoes, shirts and coats, plates, knives and forks, passports, and tins.

### III. Fill in the blanks with `some` or `any`
Practice affirmative, negative, and interrogative forms with pictures, men, glasses, magazines, ice creams, and sales reps.

## Lesson 29 - Come in, Amy.

### I. Give correct replies using the words given
Example: *The spoons and forks are dirty. (clean)* -> *Clean them, please.*

Make imperative replies for untidy beds, a hot room, blunt knives/pencils, dirty hands and face, clothes on the bed, a full cupboard, an open door, a dirty floor, a dark room, a stereo, a bookshelf, coats, cooker, windows, boxes, and a book.

### II. Translate imperative sentences into English
Practice: come in; sit down; give me an umbrella; open the window; open a book and read a lesson; show tickets; empty an ashtray and sweep the floor; put a coat in the wardrobe; clean a bicycle; put a newspaper on the table; turn on/off appliances; open a box; clean a classroom; put books on a shelf; empty a plate.

## Lesson 30 - Where's Sally?

### I. Complete questions and answers with `he`, `she`, `it`, `they`, `there`, or possessive forms
Practice locating people and things, including Sally, a dog, books, an umbrella, flowers, and household objects.

### II. Ask and answer location questions according to cues
Use singular and plural questions with *Where is/are ...?* and answers using `in`, `on`, `under`, `near`, `behind`, and `between`.

### III. Rewrite singular sentences in the plural
Change people, objects, and locations while correctly using `there are`, plural nouns, and `they are`.

## Lesson 31 - Where's Susan?

### I. Fill in the blanks with the correct preposition
Use `in`, `on`, `under`, `near`, `behind`, `between`, `beside`, and location phrases for rooms, furniture, and objects.

### II. Ask and answer using the cues
Form *Where is/are ...?* questions and state both the room and the exact position of objects and people.

### III. Complete dialogues
Practice *Where's Susan?*, *She's ...*, *Is she ...?*, and plural location questions with personal and object pronouns.

## Lesson 32 - What's he doing?

### I. Make questions and answers from the cues
Use present continuous forms: *What's he/she doing?* and *What are they doing?* Examples include reading, typing, making tea, sweeping, cleaning, sharpening, putting on/taking off clothes, and opening/shutting doors.

### II. Fill in the blanks with the present continuous
Use `am/is/are` plus `-ing` forms in positive, negative, and question sentences.

### III. Rewrite statements as questions and negatives
Practice `He is ...`, `She is ...`, `They are ...`, and short answers.

## Lesson 33 - A fine day

### I. Complete sentences with present continuous forms
Describe people and animals in a park/garden: walking, reading, sitting, running, drinking, eating, talking, and playing.

### II. Ask and answer according to picture-style cues
Use *What is he/she doing?*, *What are they doing?*, and *Who is ...?*

### III. Translate sentences into English
Practice everyday present-continuous descriptions and location phrases.

## Lesson 34 - What are they doing?

### I. Make questions from the answers
Use *What are they doing?*, *Who is/are ...?*, *Where are ...?*, and present continuous answers.

### II. Put the words in the correct order
Build present-continuous questions, short answers, and statements with singular and plural subjects.

### III. Complete the dialogues
Practice contrasts between *What is he doing?* and *What are they doing?*

## Lesson 35 - Our village

### I. Fill in the blanks with `there is`, `there are`, `is there`, or `are there`

### II. Change singular/plural descriptions
Convert `There is a ...` to `There are some ...`, and use `any` in questions and negatives.

### III. Answer questions about a village or town
Use *Is there ...?*, *Are there any ...?*, *Where is/are ...?*, and concise affirmative/negative replies.

## Lesson 41 - Penny's bag

## Lesson 42 - Is there a ...?

### I. Complete questions and answers with `there is/there are`
Use singular/plural countable nouns, `some`, `any`, and short answers.

### II. Ask about objects in rooms and containers
Form questions with *Is there a/an ...?* and *Are there any ...?*, then give location answers.

### III. Change statements into questions and negatives
Practice countable nouns and `some/any`.

## Lesson 43 - Hurry up!

### I. Give commands and warnings
Use imperative verbs and negative commands: hurry up, wait, look, come, sit, stand, open, shut, take, put, and don't.

### II. Complete dialogues
Practice direct requests, object pronouns, and responses such as *All right*, *Here you are*, and *I'm sorry.*

### III. Translate commands into English
Focus on common instructions and classroom/home vocabulary.

## Lesson 44 - Are there any ...?

### I. Fill in the blanks with `some` or `any`

Practice short answers and full replies that name the location or quantity.

### III. Rewrite sentences in singular and plural
Use `a/an`, `some`, `any`, `there is`, and `there are` correctly.

## Lesson 47 - A cup of coffee

### I. Complete requests and offers
Practice `Would you like ...?`, *Do you want ...?*, *Give me ...*, and food/drink vocabulary including coffee, tea, bread, cheese, and cigarettes.

### II. Use `some`, `any`, and quantities
Form questions, offers, positive answers, and negative answers with countable and uncountable nouns.

### III. Complete short dialogues
Practice buying/offering food and drink, specifying which one/ones, and replying politely.

## Lesson 48 - Do you like ...?

### I. Ask and answer with `like`
Use *Do you like ...?*, *Does he/she like ...?*, affirmative/negative short answers, and food/drink or activity vocabulary.

### II. Complete sentences with the correct verb form
Distinguish `like/likes`, `don't like/doesn't like`, and subject pronouns.

### III. Make questions from cues and write replies
Practice personal preferences, third-person preferences, and simple present questions/answers.
