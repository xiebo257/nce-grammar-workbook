## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Contact clause (thing) | `the book you got from the library` | The object relative pronoun `which/that` can be omitted. |
| Contact clause (person) | `the man I helped` | The object `who/whom/that` can be omitted in defining clauses. |
| Final preposition | `the chair he was sitting on` | Keep the preposition at the end of the relative clause. |
| Phrasal verb in clause | `the dress she put on` | Keep the particle with the verb, not stranded before the noun. |
| Tense inside clause | `the book he read last week` | Time adverbials stay inside the relative clause. |
| `who` subject clause | `the tourists who have already left` | Use `who` when the pronoun is the subject of the relative clause. |
| Reverse combination | `the man who is my boss is sitting in that car` | Either original sentence may be the main clause if both facts remain clear. |
| Uncountable noun | `the furniture she was cleaning` | `Furniture` has no plural; use a contact clause, not `which`. |

## Key takeaway

- In defining relative clauses, omit the object pronoun (`who`/`which`/`that`) when it is not the subject — this is the contact clause.
- Keep prepositions and phrasal-verb particles at the end of the relative clause.
- When both prompts share the same noun, either sentence can become the main clause; the worked-example pattern uses the first sentence as the main clause.
