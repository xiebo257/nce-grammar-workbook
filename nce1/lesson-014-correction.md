## Usage table

| Use | Pattern | Example | Meaning / reminder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask about an item's colour | `What colour's + noun phrase?` | `What colour's Paul's tie?` | `What's` is short for `what is`; `colour's` is short for `colour is`. |
| Ask about a person's item | `What colour's + possessive noun phrase?` | `What colour's your brother's car?` | Put the owner before the item: `your brother's car`, `Sophie's hat`. |
| Use a possessive determiner | `my/your/his/her + noun` | `your pencil`; `her handbag`; `his case` | Possessive determiners come directly before the noun. |
| Use a possessive noun | `name's + noun` | `Paul's tie`; `Anna's skirt`; `Stella's coat` | Singular names normally take `'s` to show possession. |
| Describe one item | `It's + colour.` | `It's brown.` | `It's` means `it is`; the colour adjective follows `is`. |
| Describe a combination of colours | `It's + colour + and + colour.` | `It's grey and black.` | Join two colour adjectives with `and`. |
| Show that two items match | `It's the same colour. It's + colour, too.` | `It's the same colour. It's red, too.` | `Too` means the second item has that colour as well. |
| Ask about one nearby or indicated item | `this/that + possessive noun phrase` | `this coat`; `that policeman's suit` | Keep the demonstrative and possessive phrase attached to the noun it identifies. |
| Punctuate a colour question | `What colour's ...?` | `What colour's his case?` | End the question with `?`, not a full stop. |

## Key takeaway

- Use `What colour's ...?` to ask about colour.
- Put the owner before the item: `Paul's tie`, `your son's dog`, `Sophie's hat`.
- Use `my`, `your`, `his`, or `her` before a noun: `your shirt`, `his case`.
- Answer with `It's + colour`: `It's black` or `It's brown and white`.
- For two matching items, use `It's the same colour. It's ..., too.`
- End every colour question with a question mark.
