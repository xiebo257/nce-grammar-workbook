## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Regular past tense: add `-ed` | `worked`, `washed`, `finished`, `played`, `rained`, `watched` | Most verbs: just add `-ed`. Verbs ending in `-sh`/`-ch` also take `-ed`. |
| Regular past tense: add `-d` | `arrived`, `shaved` | Verbs ending in silent `-e`: add `-d` only. |
| Regular past tense: `y`→`i`+`-ed` | `studied`, `emptied` | Consonant + `y` → change `y` to `i`, add `-ed`. Vowel + `y` → just add `-ed` (`played`). |
| Habitual vs past contrast | `Mary often walks…, but she walked… yesterday.` | First clause: present simple with `often` (+`-s`/`-es` for singular). Second clause: past simple (`-ed`/`-d`). |
| Third-person singular `-s` in habitual clause | `He often walks…` / `She often watches…` | Singular subject + `often` → verb + `-s`/`-es`. Never the bare base form. |
| `Did…?` → `Yes/No` + past simple + pronoun | `Yes, he did. He painted them. No, he didn't. He didn't wash them.` | Short answer (`did`/`didn't`) + full answer with past verb + pronoun object. |
| Pronoun substitution: singular → `it`, plural → `them` | `the door` → `it`; `the windows` → `them` | Match the pronoun to the noun's number: singular → `it`, plural → `them`. |
| Phrasal verb pronoun placement | `turn it on`, not `turn on it` | With a pronoun object, the pronoun goes **between** the verb and the particle. With a full noun, it goes after: `turn on the tap`. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q20 — third-person singular `-s`.** `He often walk` → `He often walks`. This is the same error type seen in L50 Q15, L55 Q19, L64 Q15/Q18. The learner correctly applied `-s` in Q13 (`studies`), Q14 (`stays`), Q15 (`watches`), Q16 (`shaves`), Q17 (`arrives`), Q19 (`rains`) — 6 correct instances in this same lesson. The Q20 slip is isolated.
- **Q28 — phrasal verb pronoun placement.** `turned on it` → `turned it on`. With pronoun objects, the pronoun goes **between** the verb and the particle: `turn it on`, `turn it off`, `put it on`, `take it off`. With full nouns, the noun goes after: `turn on the tap`, `turn off the cooker`. This rule was taught in Lesson 39 (three-form phrasal verb pattern).
- **Q30 — singular pronoun for plural noun.** "the windows" (plural) → `them`, not `it`. Match the pronoun to the noun's number: `the door` (singular) → `it`; `the windows` (plural) → `them`. The learner correctly used `it` for the door (Q1) but used `it` for the windows (Q2) — should be `them`.
