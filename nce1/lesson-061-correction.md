## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `Does X have a Y? (adj)` → `Yes, [pronoun] does. [Pronoun] has a [adj] Y.` | `Does Dave have a glass? (clean)` → `Yes, he does. He has a clean glass.` | Place the adjective before the noun: `a clean glass`, not `a glass clean`. |
| `Does X have a Y? (Z)` → `No, [pronoun] doesn't. [Pronoun] only has a Z.` | `Does John have a blue tie? (red tie)` → `No, he doesn't. He only has a red tie.` | Negate + state what they DO have with `only has`. |
| `Does X have any Y and Z?` → `X has some Y, but X doesn't have any Z.` | `Does Linda have any pencils and pens? She has some pencils, but she doesn't have any pens.` | `some` (affirmative), `any` (negative). Question ends with `?`. |
| `must` for obligation | `She must stay at home.` / `You must take her to a doctor.` / `I must drink more water.` | `[Subject] must + base verb`. Subject changes based on who is ill or who is advised. |
| `Because` for reasons | `Because she is hungry.` / `Because she wants an ice cream.` | `Because + [pronoun] + [reason]`. Third-person singular verbs take `-s`/`-es`: `wants`, `has`, `likes`. |
| Third-person singular in `Because` answers | `she wants`, `she has`, `she likes` | After `Because she/he/[name]`, use third-person singular: `want`→`wants`, `have`→`has`, `like`→`likes`. |
