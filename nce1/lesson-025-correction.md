## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `a`/`an` (first mention, indefinite) | `a new table`, `an ice cream`, `an American tourist` | Use for singular countable nouns introduced for the first time. `an` before a vowel sound. |
| `the` (second mention / specific) | `The refrigerator is a white one.` | Once a noun is introduced (`a refrigerator`), refer to it with `the`. |
| `the` in fixed phrases | `the middle of`, `the matter`, `the right`, `the left` | These phrases require `the` regardless of first/second mention. |
| `the` with unique/specific reference | `the kitchen`, `the table`, `the cupboard` | Both speakers share the context, so `the` marks the known item. |
| `in` vs `on` for location | `in the bottle/room/kitchen/handbag/middle`; `on the wall/desk/floor/table/right` | `on` = on a surface; `in` = enclosed/contained. `on the right/left` and `in the middle` are fixed. |
| `one` as noun substitute in `There is one …` | `There is one on the plate.` | `one` replaces the noun just negated; do **not** drop it — "There is on the plate" is ungrammatical. |
| Section III frame | `No, there isn't a/an ___ in/on ___. There is one ___. It's ___.` | Negate the question, then state the real location with `one`, then describe with the adjective cue. Every content word must match the new prompt — don't reuse a previous item's answer. |
| `What's the matter?` | `What's the matter, John?` | Fixed expression; always `the matter`, never `a matter` in this idiom. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q1 — article vs preposition.** The blank in "It's in ___ middle" needs the article `the`, not the preposition `in` (which is already printed). When a blank sits between a preposition and a noun, fill the article, not the preposition.
- **Q25 — copy-paste.** When a Section III prompt changes the noun and cues, every content word (the noun, the location, the adjective) must change with it. Reusing the previous item's answer produces a grammatically valid but wrong sentence.
- **Q28 / Q29 — `one` is not optional.** The frame requires `There is **one** on …`. The pronoun `one` stands in for the negated noun; dropping it leaves "There is on …", which has no subject complement and is ungrammatical.
- Section II (`in`/`on`) is fully correct — the surface vs contained distinction is secure, including fixed phrases `on the right`, `in the middle`.

**Summary:** 30/34; the four errors cluster around three habits — confusing a preposition already in the text with the article a blank needs (Q1), reusing a prior answer without adapting content words (Q25), and dropping the substitute pronoun `one` from the `There is one …` frame (Q28, Q29).
