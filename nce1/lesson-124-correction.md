## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Participle reduction | `the man opening the bank` | `who is opening` → `opening`; `that is on` → `on`. |
| Contact clause (object) | `the hat the nurse is wearing` | Object `who`/`whom`/`which`/`that` can be omitted in defining clauses. |
| Contact clause after indefinite pronoun | `there is little I can do` | `That` can be omitted after `little`, `something`, `all`, etc. |
| Final preposition | `the town you live in` | Keep the preposition at the end of the relative clause. |
| Phrasal-verb particle | `the coat you took off` | Keep the particle with the verb, not before the noun. |
| Extra object pronoun | `the pen he was writing with it` | Remove the redundant object pronoun when a relative clause is already present. |
| Wrong relative pronoun | `a watch who I can use` | Use `which/that` for things, `who/whom` for people. |
| Dangling participle | `some people not liking the idea` | A present participle cannot serve as a finite verb; rewrite with `who didn't like`. |
| Wrong participle tense | `the kitten Jane finding` | Use the past participle or past tense (`found`) for completed actions. |

## Key takeaway

- In defining relative clauses, omit object pronouns (`who`/`whom`/`which`/`that`) to form contact clauses.
- Reduce `who/which + be + participle` to just the participle: `who is opening` → `opening`.
- Keep prepositions and phrasal-verb particles at the end of the relative clause.
- Watch for three common sentence-level errors: extra object pronouns (`it`), dangling participles used as finite verbs, and wrong participle tense.
