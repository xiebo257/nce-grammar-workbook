## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `more/fewer + plural countable noun + than` | `more letters than Jane has` | Compare numbers of books, skirts, letters, or mistakes. |
| `more/less + uncountable noun + than` | `less bread than Ann did` | Compare an amount of bread, beer, chocolate, or steak. |
| Countable units versus substance | `fewer slices of bread` / `less bread` | Choose `fewer` for units and `less` for the substance. |
| `the most/the fewest` | `the fewest mistakes of the three` | Use superlatives for the highest or lowest count in a group. |
| `the most/the least` | `the least chocolate` | Use `least` for the smallest uncountable amount. |
| Auxiliary matching | `has bought ... than Mary has`; `ate ... than Mary did` | Match the auxiliary to the tense of the main clause. |
| `good → better → best` | `better at Science than Bob`; `the best in the class` | Use `better` with `than` and `the best` for the whole group. |
| `bad → worse → worst` | `worse at English than Sophie`; `the worst in the class` | `Worse` is comparative; `worst` is superlative. |
| `write → wrote → written` | `Jane has written the fewest letters.` | Present perfect uses `has/have + written`. |

## Key takeaway

- Use `worse ... than`, not `worst ... than`.
- Spell the past participle of `write` as `written`.
- Keep `fewer/fewest` for countable units and `less/least` for uncountable substances.
