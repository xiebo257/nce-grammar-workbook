## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Regular comparative | `fast` -> `faster` | Add `-er` to most short adjectives. |
| Regular superlative | `fast` -> `the fastest` | Add `-est` and normally use `the`. |
| Final `-y` | `happy` -> `happier` -> `the happiest` | Change consonant + `y` to `i` before `-er` or `-est`. |
| Short CVC adjective | `big` -> `bigger` -> `the biggest` | Double the final consonant before `-er` or `-est`. |
| Final silent `-e` | `nice` -> `nicer` -> `the nicest` | Add only `-r` or `-st` when the adjective already ends in `e`. |
| Group superlative | `the heaviest of all the suitcases` | Use a plural group noun after `of all the`. |
| Place superlative | `the tallest in the class` | Use `in` for a place, class, shop, house, or other containing group. |
| Experience superlative | `the nicest I've ever seen` | Use a present perfect experience clause after the superlative. |
| Subject-verb agreement | `the shoes are` | Plural subjects take `are`; singular subjects take `is`. |
| Positive statement tag | `Those apples are big, aren't they?` | A positive `are` statement takes the negative tag `aren't they?`. |
