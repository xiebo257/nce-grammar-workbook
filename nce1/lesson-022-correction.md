## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `Give + object pronoun + object, please.` | `Give me a coffee, please.` / `Give them two train tickets, please.` | The pronoun after `Give` is the **object** form (me/him/her/us/them), never the subject form (I/he/she/we/they). |
| Subject → object pronoun swap | `He` → `him`; `She` → `her`; `We` → `us`; `They` → `them` | Drop the `-s` of `wants` and switch the subject to its object form. Proper nouns (`Tony`, `Helen`, `My daughter`) also resolve to `him`/`her`. |
| Keep the original object phrase intact | `I want an egg.` → `Give me an egg, please.` | Don't change articles (`a`/`an`), quantifiers (`two`), or possessives (`his`/`their`) when you move them after the pronoun. |
| `a` vs `an` before a vowel sound | `an ice cream`, `an egg`, `an umbrella` | The article belongs to the noun, not the pronoun; preserve it exactly. |
| Plural nouns after `those` | `those umbrellas`, `those knives`, `those glasses`, `those watches` | `knife → knives` (f→ves), `glass → glasses` (ss→sses), `watch → watches` (ch→ches). Use the plural in the opening exclamation, then the singular (`your knife`, `your glass`) in the question. |
| `one` as a pronoun substitute | `the green one`, `the small one`, `the black and grey one` | `one` replaces the noun just mentioned to avoid repetition; the adjective (or compound adjective) goes before it. |
| `this one` / `that one` | `This one? No, not that one.` | Demonstrative pronouns pair with `one`; `this one` = near, `that one` = far. |
| Dialogue frame for Section II | `Look at those …! Which is your …? This one? No, not that one. My … is the … one.` | Reproduce the whole frame from the example; only the noun and the descriptive adjective change per cue. |

## Key takeaway

- Section I exercises the **object-pronoun imperative** `Give + me/him/her/us/them + (object), please.` The only transformation is the subject → object pronoun; everything after the pronoun (article, quantifier, possessive, noun) is carried over unchanged.
- Section II exercises the **`one` pronoun** and **noun plurals** inside a fixed dialogue frame: pluralise the noun in `Look at those …!` (watch -es, knife -ves, glass -ses), then substitute the adjective with `the … one`.
