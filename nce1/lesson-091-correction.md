## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Replace `am/is/are going to` with `will` | `The bus is going to take you` -> `The bus will take you` | Use `will` with `he`, `she`, `it`, `you`, and plural subjects in this exercise. Keep the main verb in its base form. |
| Traditional `shall` with `I/we` | `We're going to have a meeting` -> `We shall have a meeting` | The workbook accepts and models `shall` with first-person `I` and `we`. |
| Future contractions | `He'll do`, `I'll buy`, `She'll get up` | `I'll` = `I will`, `he'll` = `he will`, and `she'll` = `she will`. |
| Present perfect affirmative | `I've lost my umbrella.` / `My son has lost his book.` | Form: `have/has + past participle`; use `has` with `he`, `she`, and singular nouns. |
| Present perfect negative | `We haven't finished our homework yet.` | Form: `have/has + not + past participle`; `yet` commonly appears at the end for an unfinished action. |
| Past participle of `get` in `get up` | `She hasn't got up yet.` | After `has/have`, use `got` (British English), not the base form `get`. |
| Duration with present perfect | `I've had a bad stomachache for two days.` | Use `for` + a length of time to describe a condition continuing up to now. |
| Future reassurance or offer | `It doesn't matter. I'll buy you a new one.` | Use `will`/`shall` for a decision, offer, or response made in the conversation. |
