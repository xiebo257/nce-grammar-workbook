## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Present continuous: `be` + verb`-ing` | `are sitting`, `is shining`, `isn't raining` | Form: `subject + am/is/are + verb-ing`. Use `is` for singular (it, the sun, a man), `are` for plural (people, men and women, they). |
| Shared auxiliary | `They are eating ice creams or drinking tea.` | When two verbs share the same subject and auxiliary, the `are` is stated once: `are eating … or drinking`. |
| Negative present continuous | `It isn't raining.` | Form: `isn't/aren't + verb-ing`. Contraction of `is not` → `isn't`. |
| Question form | `What is the dog doing?` | Question: `Wh-word + is/are + subject + verb-ing?` The auxiliary (`is`) and the `-ing` verb (`doing`) are separated by the subject (`the dog`). |
| Plural subject pronoun | `the boys` → `they`; `the cats` → `they`; `the children` → `they` | All plural groups use `they` as the answer pronoun. |
| `you` → `I` pronoun shift | `Are you watching? — Yes, I am.` | When the question uses `you`, the answer shifts to `I`: `Yes, I am.` (not `Yes, you are.`) |
| Section II dialogue frame | `Are [group1] verb-ing? Yes, they are. Are [group2] verb-ing, too? No, they aren't. They're [verb2-ing] [object2].` | Five sentences: question → yes → parallel question → no → correction. Group2 does a different action. |
| Straight apostrophe in contractions | `They're`, `aren't`, `isn't` | Use a straight apostrophe (`'`, U+0027) in contractions. Avoid curly quotes (`'`, U+2018) or backticks. |
