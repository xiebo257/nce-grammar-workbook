## Key takeaway

- **Q7 — imperative vs past tense in direct speech.** The teacher's command "Sit down and start work" is inside quotation marks — it's a direct command, so the verbs must be in the **imperative** (base form): `Sit` (capitalized, starts the quote) and `start` (not `started`). The learner wrote `sat` and `started` — using past tense. **Rule: commands in direct speech use the base form (imperative), not the past tense. "He said, 'Sit down'" — not "He said, 'Sat down'."** The past tense is for reporting verbs (`said`, `arrived`, `gave`), not for the command itself.
