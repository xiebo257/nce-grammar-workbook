## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `too + adj + to + verb` (same subject) | `He was too young to go to school.` | When the subject of both clauses is the same person/thing, omit `for + pronoun`. |
| `too + adj + for + pronoun + to + verb` | `The box was too heavy for him to lift.` | When the subject (box) differs from the actor (he), add `for + pronoun`. |
| `adj + enough + for + pronoun + to + verb` | `The shelf is low enough for him to reach.` | Positive counterpart of the `too` pattern: `enough` means sufficient. |
| `adj + enough + to + verb` (same subject) | `The knife is sharp enough to cut the beef.` | When subject = actor, no `for + pronoun` needed. |
| Pronoun shift for listener | `You can drink it.` → `for me to drink` | When `you` in the prompt refers to the speaker, shift to `me`. |
| Pronoun shift for named person | `The old woman can eat it.` → `for her to eat` | Replace a named noun with the matching object pronoun. |
