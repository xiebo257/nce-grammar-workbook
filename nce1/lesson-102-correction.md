## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Reported speech (present reporting verb) | `He says he feels ill.` | When the reporting verb is in the present (`says`, `say`), the tense in the reported clause does not change. |
| Pronoun shift by gender | `Peter: "I feel ill."` → `Peter says he feels ill.` | Match the reported pronoun to the speaker's gender: `he` for males, `she` for females. |
| Plural pronoun shift | `The boys: "We're tired."` → `The boys say they're tired.` | Change `we` to `they` when the speakers are third-person plural. |
| Reflexive pronoun shift | `I'm enjoying myself.` → `she is enjoying herself` | Shift `myself` to `herself`/`himself` to match the speaker. |
| Future in reported speech | `I'll write.` → `she'll write` | With present `says`, keep `will` → `she'll`, `he'll`. |
| Tag question: pronoun for `these` | `These aren't your books, are they?` | Use `they` (not `these`) as the pronoun in the tag for demonstrative subjects. |
| Tag question: negative → positive | `You can't speak English, can you?` | Reverse the polarity: negative statement takes a positive tag. |
