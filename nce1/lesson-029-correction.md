## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Imperative + `them` (plural) | `Clean them, please.` / `Sharpen them, please.` | Use `them` when the object in the prompt is plural (spoons, beds, pencils, windows). |
| Imperative + `it` (singular) | `Empty it, please.` / `Dust it, please.` | Use `it` when the object in the prompt is singular (cupboard, door, knife, box, book). |
| Phrasal verb imperatives | `Turn on the light.` / `Turn off the stereo.` / `Put on your coat.` / `Take off your coat.` | The particle (on/off) comes before the object. |
| `the` with ordinals | `read the first lesson` | Ordinal numbers (first, second, third) require `the` before the noun: `the first lesson`, `the second page`. |
| `please` for `请` | `Come in, please.` / `Give me your ticket, please.` | When the Chinese source has `请`, include `please` in the English translation — either at the end (`..., please.`) or the start (`Please ...`). |
| `an` before vowel sound | `an umbrella` | `一把伞` → `an umbrella` (vowel sound). `a` before consonant sound: `a book`. |
| Imperative capitalization | `Sharpen it, please.` | The imperative verb at the start of a sentence must be capitalized. |
| Spelling: `sweep` vs `seep` | `Sweep the floor.` (clean) vs `Water seeps through cracks.` (leak) | `sweep` = clean with a broom; `seep` = leak slowly. Different words, different meanings. |
| Spelling: `light` | `Turn off the light.` | Common spelling: l-i-g-h-t. Double-check before submitting. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q25 — missing `the` with ordinal.** "first lesson" needs `the` → "the first lesson". Ordinal numbers (first, second, third) are specific references and always take `the` in English. This applies broadly: "the first page", "the second chapter", "the third exercise".
- **Q27 — spelling: `seep` vs `sweep`.** "seep" means to leak slowly; "sweep" means to clean with a broom. The missing `w` changes the word entirely. When writing verbs from the lesson vocabulary, sound out each letter.
- **Q32 — spelling: `lifght` vs `light`.** A letter-scramble. `light` is one of the most common English words — l-i-g-h-t. Slow down on high-frequency words.
- **Q4, Q26, Q34 — minor slips.** Q4: the imperative verb should be capitalized. Q26 and Q34: `请` in the Chinese source should produce `please` in the English translation. The learner demonstrates this correctly in Q21/Q22 — the pattern is known, just not consistently applied.
- **Section I — imperative + pronoun secure.** The `them`/`it` distinction for plural/singular objects is perfect across all 20 items, including phrasal verbs (`turn on`, `turn off`, `put on`, `take off`).
