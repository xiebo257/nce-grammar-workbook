## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Present passive (general) | `English is spoken all over the world` | Use `is/are + past participle` for general facts and routines. |
| Past passive | `was written ten years ago` | Use `was/were + past participle` for completed past actions. |
| Plural subject | `The doors and windows were shut` | Plural subjects take `were`. |
| Singular subject | `It was built a hundred years ago` | Singular subjects take `was`. |
| Irregular past participle | `win → won` (not `wined`) | `win` is irregular: `win/won/won`. |
| Singular `they` | `Doesn't anyone ever …? → Of course they do!` | `anyone` takes `they` for pronoun reference. |
| Emphatic `do` | `Of course they do!` | Auxiliary `do` contradicts the negative question. |
| Time cue → tense | `every week` → present; `yesterday` → past | Match passive tense to the time adverbial. |

## Key takeaway

- Match passive tense to the time cue: `every week` / `every day` → present (`is/are done`); `yesterday` / `last week` / `ago` → past (`was/were done`).
- Irregular verb forms must be memorised: `win/won/won`, not `win/wined/wined`.
- Watch spelling in past participles: `washed`, not `wahsed`.
- `Of course they do!` uses `they` (singular they for `anyone`) and emphatic `do` to push back against a negative question.
