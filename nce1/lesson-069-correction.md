## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `in` + countries/cities/months/seasons | `in Japan`, `in England`, `in London`, `in September`, `in the country` | Use `in` for enclosed places (countries, cities) and time periods (months, seasons). |
| `on` + days/dates | `on Sunday`, `on March 10th`, `on August 9th`, `on Christmas Day`, `on May 1st` | Use `on` for specific calendar days and dates — including "Christmas Day." |
| `at` + places/clock times/fixed phrases | `at church`, `at home`, `at night`, `at the moment`, `at the meeting`, `at the hairdresser's` | Use `at` for specific points (places, times, fixed expressions). |
| `at` vs `on` with Christmas | `at Christmas` (the holiday period) vs `on Christmas Day` (December 25th) | `at Christmas` = during the holiday period; `on Christmas Day` = on the specific date. "Christmas Day" is a calendar date → `on`. |
| `at` + clock time | `at about five`, `at ten o'clock`, `at noon` | Clock times take `at`: "at about five," not "about five" alone. |
| Cue expansion: place | `home` → `at home`; `the park` → `in the park`; `Austria` → `in Austria`; `church` → `at church` | When the cue gives a bare noun, add the correct preposition: `at` + home/church, `in` + country/park, `at` + shop's. |
| `or`-question answer | `Were they at A or at B? (at A)` → `They were at A.` | Select the cue option and state it with matching pronoun + was/were. |

## Key takeaway

- **Q10 — `at Christmas Day` → `on Christmas Day`.** "Christmas Day" (December 25th) is a specific calendar date → takes `on`, not `at`. The distinction: `at Christmas` (without "Day") = the holiday period; `on Christmas Day` = the specific date. **Rule: any expression containing "Day" in a date sense takes `on`: on Christmas Day, on New Year's Day, on Children's Day.**
- **Q22 — missing `at` before clock time.** "I was there about five" → "I was there **at** about five." Clock times require `at`: `at about five`, `at ten o'clock`, `at noon`. The cue shorthand "(about five)" expands to "at about five."
