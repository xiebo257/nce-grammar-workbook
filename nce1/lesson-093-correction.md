## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Future yes/no question | `Will she clean the kitchen tomorrow morning?` | Form: `Will + subject + base verb + ...?` |
| Negative future reply | `No, she won't. She cleaned it yesterday morning.` | Use `won't` for the future refusal/negative, then use past simple for the earlier action. |
| Future-to-past contrast | `Will they go next month?` -> `They went there last month.` | Change the future plan to the correct past form: `go` -> `went`, `fly` -> `flew`, `see` -> `saw`. |
| Place substitution | `They went to London` -> `They went there` | Use `there` after verbs such as `go` and `fly`; with `visit`, keep the place (`visited Seoul`) or change the verb to `went there`. |
| Object substitution | `She cleaned the kitchen` -> `She cleaned it` | Replace a specific singular object with `it`; the workbook also uses `played it` for `played football`. |
| Irregular past forms | `go` -> `went`; `fly` -> `flew`; `see` -> `saw` | Memorize these past forms; `sat` is the past of `sit`, not `see`. |
| Future dialogue with `shall` | `I shall go to Moscow next Friday.` | The workbook models `shall` with `I/we`; `will` and contractions are also used with other subjects. |
| Viewpoint shifts | `you` -> `I`; `your` -> `my`; `with you` -> `with me` | Rewrite from the answerer's perspective: `I'll clean my room`; `My friend will go with me`. |
| Cue fidelity | `(tomorrow evening)` -> `tomorrow evening` | Copy every supplied time cue exactly; Q17 changed `evening` to `morning`, so its answer is not complete. |
