## Usage table

| Use | Pattern | Example | Meaning / reminder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ask about ownership | `Whose + noun + is + this/that?` | `Whose tie is that?` | `Whose` means "belonging to whom" and comes before the noun. |
| Ask about ownership with the noun after a demonstrative | `Whose + is + this/that + noun?` | `Whose is this white car?` | `Whose` stands alone; `this` or `that` comes before the noun. |
| Ask about facts or identity | `What + noun + is ...?` | `What nationality is he?` / `What colour is the dress?` | Use `what` before the information category: nationality, colour, make, name, or job. |
| Ask about a person's condition | `How + be + subject?` | `How are you?` / `How is Helen?` | `How` asks about condition, well-being, or manner. Match `is`/`are` to the subject. |
| Ask about an occupation | `What is + possessive noun phrase + job?` | `What is your father's job?` | `What do you do?` is also a common way to ask about someone's occupation. |
| Point to one nearby item | `This is + noun phrase.` | `This is my book.` | `This` identifies one item near the speaker. |
| Point to one more distant item | `That is + noun phrase.` | `That is Emma's car.` | `That` identifies one item farther away or already indicated. |
| Show ownership before a noun | `my/your/his/her + noun`; `name's + noun` | `your son's pencil`; `Emma's car` | Possessive determiners and possessive nouns come before the thing owned. |
| Describe the item | `It is + adjective.` | `It's clean.` / `It's yellow.` | `It's` is the contraction of `it is`; the adjective follows `is`. |

## Key takeaway

- Use `Whose` for ownership: `Whose is this umbrella?`
- Use `What` for facts or categories: `What nationality ...?`, `What colour ...?`, `What make ...?`, and `What is ...?`
- Use `How` for condition or well-being: `How are you?`, `How is your mother?`
- Use `This` and `That` before a singular noun phrase: `This is my book`; `That is his suit`.
- Put possessive words before the noun: `your chair`, `your son's pencil`, `Emma's car`.
- Use `It's + adjective` to describe the item: `It's clean`, `It's red`.
