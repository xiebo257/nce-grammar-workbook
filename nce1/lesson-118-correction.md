## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Background action | `was doing her housework` | Use past continuous for an action already in progress. |
| Interrupting event | `the telephone rang` | Use past simple for a completed event. |
| Simultaneous actions | `were playing / was cooking` | Two ongoing actions may both use past continuous. |
| Past continuous | `was studying` | Include `was/were`; `studying` alone is incomplete. |
| Spelling after short vowel | `begin → beginning` | Double the final consonant before `-ing`. |
| Uncountable `news` | `the news` | Do not say `a news`. |
| `Knock` preposition | `knock on/at the door` | Do not use `knock the door`. |

## Key takeaway

- Choose past simple for the shorter completed event and past continuous for the ongoing background action.
- A continuous form needs `was/were + verb-ing`.
- Match open-ended completions to their time markers and use complete continuous forms.
