## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `had better` advice | `You'd better sit down.` | Form: `subject + had better + base verb`; `had` is commonly contracted: `you'd`, `he'd`, `we'd`. |
| Phrasal verb with a pronoun object | `Your coat is dirty.` -> `You'd better take it off.` | With a pronoun, place it between the verb and particle: `take it off`, not `take off it`; do not omit the object. |
| `must` obligation | `He must go and see a doctor.` | Form: `subject + must + base verb`; do not add `to` or an `-s` ending after `must`. |
| Subject matching | `I'm very hungry.` -> `I must have a meal.` | Keep the subject from the statement unless the cue explicitly supplies another subject. |
| Activity collocations | `have a cup of tea`, `have a rest`, `have a haircut`, `have a meal` | `have` combines with common activities and meals; keep the complete collocation. |
| `plenty of` with countable nouns | `plenty of books`, `plenty of people`, `plenty of ties` | Use `plenty of + plural countable noun` for a large quantity. |
| `plenty of` with uncountable nouns | `plenty of money`, `plenty of water`, `plenty of time`, `plenty of milk` | The same phrase also works with uncountable nouns; no plural ending is added. |
| `a lot of` -> `plenty of` | `There are a lot of books.` -> `There are plenty of books.` | Replace only the quantity phrase and preserve the rest of the sentence, including tense and agreement. |
