## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `was` (singular past `be`) | `It was cold.` / `I was at school.` / `Susan was in the country.` | Singular subjects (`I`, `he`, `she`, `it`, [name], [singular noun]) → `was`. |
| `were` (plural past `be`) | `John and Paul were busy.` / `Were you at home?` | Plural subjects (`we`, `you`, `they`, [plural noun]) → `were`. `you` always takes `were` even when singular. |
| Short answer: `Yes, [pronoun] was.` | `Yes, it was.` / `Yes, she was.` / `Yes, I was.` | Echo `was` with matching pronoun: `you`→`I`, `it`→`it`, `he`→`he`, `she`→`she`. |
| Short answer: `No, [pronoun] wasn't.` | `No, I wasn't.` / `No, she wasn't.` | Echo `wasn't` (contraction of `was not`) with matching pronoun. |
| Affirmative + negative + correction | `Yes, I was. No, she wasn't. She was at the office.` | Line 1: affirm for Q1. Line 2: negate for Q2. Line 3: state where Q2's subject actually was. |
| Double negative with `either` | `No, I wasn't. No, he wasn't, either.` | Line 1: negate Q1. Line 2: negate Q2 with `, either` to show "also not." |
| Pronoun gender for female names | Jill → `she`; Ann → `she`; Jane → `she`; Lisa → `she` | Female names take `she`. The learner correctly used `she` for Ann (Q31), Jane (Q25), Lisa (Q33), and `your sister` (Q30) — but missed it for Jill (Q28). |
