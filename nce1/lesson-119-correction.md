## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Past perfect | `had + past participle` | It marks the earlier of two past actions. |
| `come` forms | `come–came–come` | After `had`, use `come`. |
| `eat` forms | `eat–ate–eaten` | Use `had eaten`, not `had ate`. |
| `drink` forms | `drink–drank–drunk` | Use `had drunk`, not `had drank`. |
| Double `had` | `had had breakfast` | The first `had` is auxiliary; the second is the participle of `have`. |
| Plural possessive | `the students' papers` | For a regular plural ending in `s`, add the apostrophe after `s`. |
| Time clause | `When the rain had stopped, ...` | Use `when` to join the earlier and later actions. |

## Key takeaway

- Build the past perfect with `had + past participle`, especially `had come`, `had eaten`, and `had drunk`.
- When one `had` governs coordinated verbs, both verbs need past participles: `had eaten ... and drunk ...`.
