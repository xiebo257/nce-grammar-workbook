## Usage table

| Pattern | Example from this lesson | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Yes/no agreement | `Are you tired? Yes, I am.` | Make the answer agree with the question: `yes` pairs with an affirmative form, `no` with a negative form. |
| Plural short answers | `Are the boys tall? Yes, they are.` | Use `they are` for plural people or things. |
| `a` and `an` | `an air hostess`, `a policeman`, `an open shop` | Choose the article by the following word's sound. |
| There is + singular noun | `There's an old Ford.` | Use `There is/There's` with one person or thing. |
| Imperatives | `Please sit down.` | Begin a command with the base verb; add `please` for politeness. |
| Singular and plural nouns | `my friend` / `my friends` | Keep the noun number aligned with the Chinese prompt and intended meaning. |
| Questions stay questions | `Are they hairdressers?` | When rewriting in the plural, preserve the original sentence type. |
| Plural `be` agreement | `My teachers are old.` | Use `are` with plural subjects such as `teachers`, `parents`, and `they`. |

## Key takeaway

- Match `yes` or `no` with the verb form that follows; `Yes, I'm not` is contradictory.
- Use `an` before vowel sounds: `an open shop`.
- Preserve singular/plural meaning in translation: `my friend` is not `my friends`.
- Keep the required wording and sentence type when completing an exercise.
- Use `are` with plural subjects such as `teachers`.
