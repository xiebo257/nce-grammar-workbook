# Oral practice, speaking tests, and correction

**Speaking is required in every lesson. Every teaching unit includes an oral test, teacher correction, a new attempt, and delayed review.** A completed written workbook cannot replace this cycle.

This file makes the oral assessment procedure explicit for the [Lessons 1–48 plan](README.md). It applies to [all 24 units](units-001-024.md), including units whose written exercises you previously answered correctly.

## Our speaking routine

| Stage | What you do | What I do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hear and notice | Listen to a short model and identify its message. | Provide or select the model; draw attention to one useful sound or stress feature. |
| Guided speech | Repeat a meaningful phrase, then change one detail. | Check the target form and give brief, immediate help. |
| Unscripted speech | Answer, ask questions, and complete a task without a written answer. | Respond as the other speaker and record useful correction points. |
| First oral test | Produce a short recording or take part in an audio exchange with unseen details. | Assess the performance before showing model answers. |
| Correction | Listen to/read the feedback and practise the most important repairs. | Give specific corrections tied to your actual response. |
| Second attempt | Speak again using a different object, person, or situation. | Check whether you can apply the repair independently. |
| Delayed check | Complete a short fresh task about a week later. | Check retention and decide what needs more practice. |

During oral parts, speak before typing. Notes can contain a few content words, but a written script is not allowed for the spontaneous test. Reading aloud remains useful as a separate pronunciation exercise; label it as reading, not spontaneous speaking.

### Home oral practice: 8–12 minutes

1. **2 minutes:** listen to a short familiar passage; repeat it in meaningful chunks. Shadowing is optional once you understand it and can repeat it comfortably.
2. **2 minutes:** answer several questions without seeing answers. Change the object or person each time.
3. **2 minutes:** ask your own questions; practise both roles in a short exchange.
4. **2–3 minutes:** record a fresh response, then listen back once. Notice one missing word, unclear sound, or long hesitation.
5. **1–3 minutes:** repair the target and record a different example. Keep the first attempt so we can compare it with the second.

These minutes fit within the main plan's daily home-practice allowance; alternate the remaining reading and writing work across days. If a sound cannot be assessed from the available audio, continue with a language target rather than guess.

## The oral test for every unit

Reserve approximately **8–10 minutes of Meeting B** for the first test and initial feedback, within its interaction and exit-check time. The learner's recorded answers are much shorter than the whole test. Use the next meeting's retrieval slot for the fresh attempt and home practice for targeted rehearsal. If more correction is needed, shorten repetitive workbook practice or add a focused session.

### Part 1: listening and response

I give five short spoken prompts with the text hidden. You respond orally or choose an object and say an appropriate response. At least one prompt changes a detail from the original lesson. I score understanding separately from the grammatical form of your answer.

This part requires spoken prompts that you have not first read. If the platform supports only text, it becomes a reading-and-speaking task and **listening stays not assessed**. Merely reading a question aloud to yourself does not produce an independent listening test.

### Part 2: questions and interaction

We complete a 6–8 turn task within the unit's language range. You initiate at least two questions and take both roles across the test or retest. I introduce one change, such as the wrong object, an unavailable item, or a changed activity. The task should require understanding and choosing language.

For asynchronous audio, I can give one prompt at a time and you send a response; the next prompt depends on your answer. If you submit both roles in a single recording, I can assess those spoken lines, but I will mark live turn-taking as untested.

### Part 3: independent speech

| Stage of the course | Typical speaking task | Approximate length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lessons 1–8 | Short ownership or introduction exchanges. | 20–40 seconds across several turns. |
| Lessons 9–24 | Describe people/items and complete a clarification exchange. | 30–60 seconds. |
| Lessons 25–40 | Describe a room or scene and explain a simple plan. | 45–90 seconds. |
| Lessons 41–48 | Explain supplies or abilities and handle refreshments/preferences. | 60–90 seconds. |

These are guides, not minimum-speed requirements. A shorter clear response that completes the task can be better than padded speech. No score depends on imitating a native accent or eliminating every pause.

An optional separate reading-aloud sample can help diagnose sounds or phrasing. Success on reading aloud does not replace Parts 2 and 3.

## What to send me

On your Mac, run `./audio/record_oral.sh --lesson 1 --mode test` from the repository root. Press Enter to start, then `q` or Ctrl+C to stop and save. Copy the recording path printed by the script into our chat. See the [recorder instructions](../../audio/README.md) for microphone selection, task labels, and retries.

Send the actual recording as an audio attachment or provide its accessible local path. A common format such as M4A, MP3, or WAV is suitable for submission; decoding support will be checked when the file arrives. Use one clear speaker, an ordinary speaking volume, and a reasonably quiet place. A phone recording is enough.

Include a short label:

```text
Unit / lessons:
Task or prompt:
Attempt: 1 / retry / 7-day check
Did I read a script? yes / no
Did I use notes? none / a few keywords / full sentences
Optional: the sound or sentence I find difficult
```

A transcript is optional. I will not require you to transcribe the recording before getting feedback. If you do supply text, I will distinguish what you intended to say from what is actually audible.

For an initial diagnostic, send the **first understandable attempt**, rather than editing many takes into a perfect performance. For deliberate pronunciation practice, repeated takes are encouraged and should be labelled practice.

## How I will correct your oral test

1. **Check access and task conditions.** Confirm that the audio can be opened and that it matches the prompt. If I cannot listen to the audio in the available environment, I will say so explicitly; transcript-based feedback will cover language only.
2. **Establish what you actually said.** Listen and, where useful, use a draft transcription as an aid. Mark unclear words as uncertain. Speech recognition can normalise an error or mishear a correct word; its output alone is not evidence of your exact wording or pronunciation.
3. **Check meaning and task completion.** Did you answer the question, choose the appropriate form, and make yourself understood? Did you ask the required questions?
4. **Correct language.** Identify grammar, word choice, missing words, reference, and context-appropriate phrasing. Separate actual errors from optional more natural alternatives.
5. **Assess speech from the audio.** Where the recording can be heard clearly, check sounds, endings, word stress, sentence stress, phrase grouping, rhythm, and relevant intonation. Note any recurring problem that affects understanding.
6. **Give a usable model and repair task.** Provide a corrected sentence, a short explanation, and one practical way to practise it. Supply a spoken model when audio output is available; otherwise use stress/chunk markings and concise pronunciation guidance without pretending written notation is audio.
7. **Retest.** Ask you to use the same target with new content. A successful repetition of my sentence is useful practice, but the fresh response checks learning.

I will not infer pronunciation from spelling, invent an IPA transcription of a sound I cannot hear, or treat an automatic transcript as a pronunciation score. If a segment is ambiguous, I will give the plausible readings and request a short repeat of that segment when necessary.

### Your correction report

Reports can be saved under `nce1/teaching-plan/oral-reviews/YYYY-MM-DD-unit-XX-attempt-N.md` once a real recording has been assessed. No report or score is prefilled for a test you have not taken.

Each report contains:

- **Task result:** what you communicated successfully, and whether help was needed.
- **Evidence:** a faithful transcript or the relevant excerpts, with uncertain words marked. Include timestamps only when checked against the actual audio.
- **Corrections:** your wording → corrected wording → brief explanation in English or Chinese.
- **Pronunciation feedback:** the audible segment, the target, why it matters, and a practical exercise. State “not assessed” for unavailable or unclear evidence.
- **Natural model:** a concise improved version that preserves your intended meaning and stays within the lesson's level.
- **Priority practice:** normally two or three targets, with any additional language corrections listed for reference.
- **Retest:** fresh questions or a changed scenario, followed by review of your second attempt.

Example of the **format only**, not a claim about your speech:

| Evidence | Correction / model | Why | Fresh repair task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| If a learner says `She cooking now.` | `She's cooking now.` | The present continuous needs `is` before `cooking`. | Describe a different person reading now. |
| If a learner says `Can you to help me?` | `Can you help me?` | Use the base verb after `can`. | Ask someone to open a window. |
| If `/θ/` in `thank` is audibly unclear | Practise `thank` → `thank you` → the complete exchange. | Check tongue placement and airflow, then return to meaningful speech. | Thank someone after a new ownership exchange. |

An acceptable sentence receives no invented correction. For example, `I'm reading your book` is correct when the book belongs to the listener. The intended ownership must be known before changing `your` to `my`.

## Assessment and the second attempt

Keep the five dimensions separate so feedback is useful:

| Dimension | What the evidence should show |
| --- | --- |
| Task completion and interaction | You convey the intended message, respond to the other speaker, and ask appropriate questions. |
| Grammar and word choice | You use the unit's patterns accurately in new combinations. |
| Intelligibility | A listener can understand your words; recurring sounds or endings do not obscure essential meaning. |
| Stress, phrasing, and fluency | You group words meaningfully and keep the exchange moving at a manageable pace. |
| Listening response | You understand unseen spoken prompts and respond to their actual meaning. |

For each dimension, record **independent**, **occasional prompt**, **needs guided practice**, or **not assessed**. Add the main plan's numeric item counts where the task supplies them. Do not invent one total score by counting unassessed audio skills as passed or failed.

After feedback, practise the top targets briefly, then submit a fresh response. I compare attempts for the corrected feature and overall meaning. After about seven days, a short unseen prompt checks retention. A target is considered retained only after independent success in that delayed task; the calendar alone does not establish mastery.

## Your first oral diagnostic

**Record 45–90 seconds without writing a script.** Use simple English you already know. If a part is unfamiliar, say what you can; this diagnostic helps choose our starting point.

1. Introduce yourself or a fictional person: name and job, and optionally nationality.
2. Describe two or three objects around you and say where they are.
3. Say what you are doing now and one thing you are going to do later.
4. Say one thing you can do and one food or drink you like.
5. Ask me two questions: one about an object's ownership and one about a food or drink preference.

I will correct that actual recording before giving you a fresh follow-up task. This first recording assesses spoken production. Listening and spontaneous turn-taking will be checked separately through spoken prompts when audio interaction is available.
