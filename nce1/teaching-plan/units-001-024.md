# Twenty-four teaching units: Lessons 1–48

Use the two-meeting routine and assessment criteria in [the main plan](README.md). Each unit below supplies the specific content for that routine. **A** is the odd lesson: situation, listening, dialogue, and interaction. **B** is the even lesson: extension pictures, pattern practice, and writing. Source images and filenames are indexed in [the source map](source-map.md).

The listening questions and practical missions below are teacher-designed prompts, not a transcription of the textbook's questions. Give task instructions in Chinese if necessary without requiring the learner to produce untaught English. Use the photographed odd-lesson dialogue or passage for the first listening and the even-lesson pictures for extension. For reading transfer, prepare 3–6 new lines using only taught patterns and familiar words, changing names, objects, or locations.

Model first, guide a few attempts, and then remove support. Unless stated otherwise, each exit task uses new combinations of already taught language. Refer to the matching HTML workbook only after oral practice; select enough questions to test the target rather than require every repetitive item. Cycle through the remaining photographed vocabulary and pictures during spaced review.

**Every unit requires oral evidence.** Use its exit mission as the speaking-test scenario, follow the [oral correction procedure](oral-practice-and-tests.md), and retest after feedback with new details. Keep listening, pronunciation, and spoken fluency explicitly unassessed until the required audio evidence is available. Include the main plan's three-minute number drill using the numbers on that even lesson's pictures.

## Unit 01 · Lessons 1–2 · Excuse me! / Is this your ...?

**Can-do:** attract someone's attention, check whether an object belongs to them, request repetition, and thank them.

**Language:** `Excuse me.` `Is this your handbag?` `Pardon?` `Yes, it is.` `Thank you very much.` Learn the nouns in object-question chunks; prioritise `is`, `this`, `your`, and `it`.

**A — listen and interact:** hear the conversation with the words covered; identify what the man is checking and whether the woman owns it. Hear `Yes?` as a response to being addressed and `Yes, it is` as confirmation. Repeat, read in roles, then reconstruct with pictures.

**B — practise and transfer:** use a book, pen, pencil, and bag. Model one ownership question, then let the learner ask it about new objects. For this first unit, use an all-owned set so affirmative answers are meaningful. Introduce the negative systematically in Unit 02. Include a deliberate need for `Pardon?`.

**Sound / literacy:** distinguish `/ð/` in `this` from `/θ/` in `thank`; stress the important object word. After speaking, copy the dialogue accurately and write three new ownership questions. Use [L1](../lesson-001.html) and [L2](../lesson-002.html) selectively; the workbook's name question can wait until Unit 04.

**Exit / recycle:** return three different objects through short exchanges and initiate two questions without a script. See the [full teaching script](lesson-001-002.md).

## Unit 02 · Lessons 3–4 · Sorry, sir. / Is this your ...?

**Can-do:** identify a mistaken object, correct ownership, and apologise.

**Language:** `This is not my umbrella.` `This isn't my umbrella.` `Is this your ...?` `No, it isn't.` `Here's my ticket.` Contrast `my` and `your` according to the current speaker.

**A:** listen for which umbrella is wrong. Use two objects with owner cards; answer positive and negative questions, then reverse roles. Connect `Sorry` to an actual mistake and `Excuse me` to getting attention.

**B:** run a cloakroom exchange. The attendant deliberately offers the wrong item once; the learner rejects it, identifies the correct one, and completes the exchange. Shuffle the order of yes/no trials.

**Sound / literacy:** hear full forms and contractions, especially `isn't`; keep the final consonants audible without adding an extra syllable. Read a new cloakroom dialogue and identify the owner. Write four exchanges, including two negative responses. Use [L3](../lesson-003.html) and [L4](../lesson-004.html).

**Exit / recycle:** resolve two mix-ups, then swap roles. Revisit Unit 01's attention, repetition, and thanks. Do not accept memorised `Yes, it is` when the ownership card says otherwise.

## Unit 03 · Lessons 5–6 · Nice to meet you. / What make is it?

**Can-do:** introduce someone, say their nationality, and identify a car's make.

**Language:** `This is ...` `He's ...` `She's ...` `Is he/she ...?` `What make is it?` `It's a ...` Distinguish a nationality adjective from a name or brand.

**A:** listen to the introductions and match people to nationality cards. Model `This is Anna. She's German.` Ask confirmation questions; the learner then introduces two fictional visitors.

**B:** use the photographed car pictures and known brands for a guessing exchange. A partner has information the learner needs; the learner must ask `What make is it?` rather than simply repeat labels.

**Sound / literacy:** practise the stressed syllable in nationality words and the difference between `he's` and `she's`. Read three short visitor cards and identify a deliberately mismatched description. Write introductions for two people and two car descriptions. Use [L5](../lesson-005.html) and [L6](../lesson-006.html).

**Exit / recycle:** introduce an unfamiliar card accurately and ask about a car. Revisit `Is this your ...?` while welcoming a visitor. Ask about identity; do not infer nationality from appearance.

## Unit 04 · Lessons 7–8 · Are you a teacher? / What's your job?

**Can-do:** exchange a name, nationality, and occupation.

**Language:** `I'm ...` `Are you ...?` `Yes, I am.` `No, I'm not.` `What's your job?` `I'm a/an ...` Contrast `I'm Chinese` with `I'm an engineer`.

**A:** listen for the speakers' nationalities and jobs. Model the interview, then change one fact so the learner must give a negative answer. Use fictional cards if the learner prefers not to share personal details.

**B:** each partner receives a different identity card. Ask enough questions to complete the missing profile, then introduce that person using Unit 03 language.

**Sound / literacy:** practise contractions and articles in whole phrases, including `an engineer`; choose `a/an` by sound. Read a short profile and correct two wrong statements. Write a four-sentence self-introduction or fictional profile. Use [L7](../lesson-007.html) and [L8](../lesson-008.html).

**Exit / recycle:** conduct an interview in both roles without reading. Complete the Lessons 1–8 cumulative mission. Known vocabulary can be expanded to the learner's real job as a clearly modelled personal addition.

## Unit 05 · Lessons 9–10 · How are you today? / Look at ...

**Can-do:** greet someone, ask how they are, and describe a person's state.

**Language:** `How are you?` `I'm fine, thanks.` `How is ...?` `He's/She's ...` `Look at ...` Use the pictured adjectives in statements and questions.

**A:** hear the greeting and identify who is being discussed. Practise greeting, response, return question, and goodbye as an exchange with a purpose.

**B:** use the expression/state pictures from Lesson 10. Describe one privately selected picture so the teacher can find it. Reverse roles and include a plausible incorrect guess.

**Sound / literacy:** contrast question intonation with a complete answer; keep the adjective prominent. Briefly practise any difficult sound in the actual adjective set. Read a four-line greeting with changed names, then write a similar exchange plus two picture descriptions. Use [L9](../lesson-009.html) and [L10](../lesson-010.html).

**Exit / recycle:** sustain a greeting without a script and describe four mixed pictures. Contrast `How are you?` with `What's your job?` so the answer matches the question's meaning.

## Unit 06 · Lessons 11–12 · Is this your shirt? / Whose is this ...? Whose is that ...?

**Can-do:** find the owner of clothing and distinguish nearby from more distant objects.

**Language:** `Whose shirt is this?` `It's Tim's.` `his`, `her`, `my`, `your`; `this/that`. Connect possessive `’s` to an explicit owner.

**A:** listen to identify the shirt's owner. Place name cards beside clothes, model an ownership question, and then remove the visible owner of one item.

**B:** run lost property. The learner asks a yes/no question, then a `Whose ...?` question when needed. Change who is speaking while keeping the owner fixed, so pronouns follow meaning.

**Sound / literacy:** hear `whose` versus `who's` in context: they sound alike but do different jobs. Practise possessive endings in whole phrases. Read owner labels and check claims; write four labelled possessions and two questions. Use [L11](../lesson-011.html) and [L12](../lesson-012.html).

**Exit / recycle:** return four items correctly, including one `his` and one `her` item. Use proximity genuinely for `this/that`; avoid treating them as interchangeable vocabulary words.

## Unit 07 · Lessons 13–14 · A new dress / What colour is your ...?

**Can-do:** ask about colours, describe an item, and identify its owner.

**Language:** `What colour is your ...?` `It's ...` `a green hat`, `a new dress`. Contrast `The hat is green` with `It's a green hat`.

**A:** listen for the dress and hat colours. Match descriptions to the pictures, then ask the teacher about a hidden colour card.

**B:** describe an outfit or match luggage to its owner using colour clues. Start with one adjective before adding a second familiar one; do not introduce a complete adjective-order chart.

**Sound / literacy:** practise stress in phrases such as `a GREEN hat`; reduce the article naturally while keeping the noun clear. Read three item descriptions and select the correct picture. Write four descriptions and a short clothes dialogue. Use [L13](../lesson-013.html) and [L14](../lesson-014.html).

**Exit / recycle:** identify three items by colour and ownership without pointing. Revisit `Whose ...?`, `this/that`, and `his/her`; colour alone should not make every answer obvious.

## Unit 08 · Lessons 15–16 · Your passports, please. / Are you ...?

**Can-do:** speak about more than one traveller and more than one possession.

**Language:** `Are you ...?` `We are ...` `Are these your ...?` `Yes, they are.` `These/Those are ...` Learn singular/plural agreement in messages.

**A:** listen for the travellers' nationality and which cases belong to them. Use fictional travel cards and bags; no real identity documents are needed.

**B:** move between one traveller and a group, and between one bag and several. The learner checks ownership at a travel desk and then takes the attendant role.

**Sound / literacy:** hear plural endings `/s/`, `/z/`, and `/ɪz/` through familiar nouns such as `books`, `bags`, and `cases`. Read two luggage labels and resolve a mismatch. Write paired singular/plural exchanges. Use [L15](../lesson-015.html) and [L16](../lesson-016.html).

**Exit / recycle:** complete the Lessons 1–16 mission. Require meaning-based changes across `this is` and `these are`, and across `I` and `we`, rather than a mechanical list of plural spellings.

## Unit 09 · Lessons 17–18 · How do you do? / What are their jobs?

**Can-do:** introduce colleagues and describe a group's occupations.

**Language:** `These are ...` `What are their jobs?` `They're ...` `our/your/their`; plural occupations and relevant irregular nouns.

**A:** listen and match people to jobs in the office introduction. Reconstruct introductions using picture cards. Explain the formal greeting `How do you do?` as a formula rather than a question about health.

**B:** create a small fictional team. The learner introduces each group and asks about another team. Include a correction to a wrongly assigned occupation.

**Sound / literacy:** practise plural endings and hear `man/men`, `woman/women`. Read a team list and check descriptions. Write a five-sentence team introduction and retrieve selected difficult plurals after a delay. Use [L17](../lesson-017.html) and [L18](../lesson-018.html).

**Exit / recycle:** introduce three groups accurately. Recheck `postmen` and `housewives` from the earlier correction report. Recognise historical occupation labels; optional current alternatives such as `flight attendant` should be explicitly introduced.

## Unit 10 · Lessons 19–20 · Tired and thirsty / Look at them!

**Can-do:** describe how people feel and respond appropriately to a need.

**Language:** `What's the matter?` `We're tired/thirsty.` `Are they ...?` `Look at them.` Practise the pictured adjective contrasts and subject/object pronouns.

**A:** listen for what the children need and how the adult responds. Use facial expressions or pictures to support meaning. Ask about one person, then the group.

**B:** choose a needs card and a matching response or object. The listener selects what would help, and the speaker confirms. Keep new request forms modelled rather than silently assumed.

**Sound / literacy:** practise clear adjective endings and stress on contrasting states. Read two short situations and select a suitable response. Write three short needs dialogues using both singular and plural subjects. Use [L19](../lesson-019.html) and [L20](../lesson-020.html).

**Exit / recycle:** describe four new pictured situations and respond sensibly. Contrast `They are tired` with `Look at them`; recycle colours and possession in identifying whose drink or seat is being discussed.

## Unit 11 · Lessons 21–22 · Which book? / Give me/him/her/us/them a ...

**Can-do:** request an item, clarify which one, and give it to the right person.

**Language:** `Give me a book, please.` `Which one?` `The blue one.` `This one?` `No, not that one.` Practise `me/him/her/us/them` with visible recipient cards.

**A:** listen for the requested book and the rejected choice. Use two similar objects so clarification is genuinely necessary.

**B:** distribute objects to different people. The speaker knows the required item and recipient; the listener must ask for enough information to choose correctly. Use the adjective pairs shown in Lesson 22.

**Sound / literacy:** stress the contrast in `the BLUE one`, and keep `one` when it replaces the noun. Read a short request and select item plus recipient. Write four request-and-clarification exchanges. Use [L21](../lesson-021.html) and [L22](../lesson-022.html).

**Exit / recycle:** deliver four items correctly, with at least two clarification questions. Explain that imperatives with `please` fit some familiar contexts; later, Unit 23 adds `Can you ...?` for a softer request.

## Unit 12 · Lessons 23–24 · Which glasses? / Give me/him/her/us/them some ...

**Can-do:** ask for several items and distinguish one item from a set.

**Language:** `Give me some glasses, please.` `Which ones?` `These/Those ...` Contrast `a ... / one` with `some ... / ones`.

**A:** listen to identify the correct glasses. Start with a single item, then a set, so the difference changes the request.

**B:** set a table or distribute classroom supplies. Mix requests for one object and several. The learner asks clarification questions and checks recipients.

**Sound / literacy:** pronounce plural endings, especially `glasses`, without losing `ones`. Read a short supply request and find two selection mistakes. Write one singular and one plural exchange, then a small supply list. Use [L23](../lesson-023.html) and [L24](../lesson-024.html).

**Exit / recycle:** complete the Lessons 1–24 mission with singular/plural choices, recipient pronouns, greetings, and needs. Keep `some` practice tied to the requested quantity; its fuller contrast with `any` comes later.

## Unit 13 · Lessons 25–26 · Mrs. Smith's kitchen / Where is it?

**Can-do:** introduce objects in a room and explain where each is.

**Language:** `There is a ...` `The ... is ...` `Where is it?` `in/on`; location phrases in the source such as `on the right`, `on the left`, and `in the middle`.

**A:** listen to the kitchen description and place object cards. Distinguish introducing an object from referring back to that known object.

**B:** give directions for arranging a simple room. The teacher cannot see the learner's arrangement and asks questions; then swap roles. Use clear shared landmarks.

**Sound / literacy:** hear `there's` and `it's` as different messages, and practise the chunks `on the right` and `in the middle`. Read a room description and draw it; write five sentences about a different arrangement. Use [L25](../lesson-025.html) and [L26](../lesson-026.html).

**Exit / recycle:** locate five items and introduce at least two with `There is`. Recheck articles and complete reference phrases from the learner's earlier written errors; recycle colour and `Which one?`.

## Unit 14 · Lessons 27–28 · Mrs. Smith's living room / Where are they?

**Can-do:** describe a furnished room using singular and plural objects.

**Language:** `There are some ...` `Where are they?` `They're ...` `near`, `on`, and other location phrases in the photographs. Contrast existence with location.

**A:** listen and identify where the furniture and small objects are. Start with one chair and then several so agreement carries meaning.

**B:** use two room drawings with three differences. The learner asks questions to find the differences without seeing the other drawing. A spoken description should allow the listener to arrange objects correctly.

**Sound / literacy:** practise `there are`, plural noun endings, and stress on the location that distinguishes the pictures. Read a six-line description and correct a drawing. Write a six-sentence description using both `there is` and `there are`. Use [L27](../lesson-027.html) and [L28](../lesson-028.html).

**Exit / recycle:** identify all three differences and use singular/plural agreement independently. Revisit first mention `a/some`, later reference `the`, and short ownership exchanges.

## Unit 15 · Lessons 29–30 · Come in, Amy. / What must I do?

**Can-do:** follow household instructions and ask what needs doing.

**Language:** `Open ...` `Shut ...` `Put ...` `What must I do?` `You must ...` Use the source's action phrases as whole chunks.

**A:** listen for the jobs Amy is asked to do. Demonstrate safe actions using real objects or mime; the learner acts before being asked to explain the grammar.

**B:** a “room ready” task: one partner knows the required arrangement, the other follows instructions and asks for the next job. Reverse roles. Model `must + base verb` briefly after several meaningful examples.

**Sound / literacy:** make the verb clear in an instruction and practise final consonants in words such as `shut`. Read a short task list and put pictures in order. Write four instructions and two `must` exchanges. Use [L29](../lesson-029.html) and [L30](../lesson-030.html).

**Exit / recycle:** carry out five instructions and give three accurately. Reuse location language. Discuss how commands sound in context; `please` helps, but tone and relationship also matter.

## Unit 16 · Lessons 31–32 · Where's Sally? / What's he/she/it doing?

**Can-do:** say where someone is and what they are doing now.

**Language:** `Where is ...?` `What's she doing?` `She's ...-ing.` `Is he ...-ing?` Contrast a state/location with an action: `She's in the kitchen. She's cooking.`

**A:** listen to locate Sally and identify her activity. Use the source picture, then mime a different action and ask the learner to describe it.

**B:** play a guessing game using actions. The learner asks a yes/no question, then a `What ... doing?` question. Change person as well as action; do not infer the pronoun from a name alone when the context is unclear.

**Sound / literacy:** hear the contracted `is` and the `-ing` ending. Read activity captions and match them to pictures. Write a short “at home now” description. Practise `running`, `putting`, and `making` as relevant spelling patterns. Use [L31](../lesson-031.html) and [L32](../lesson-032.html).

**Exit / recycle:** complete the Lessons 1–32 mission. Watch for missing or doubled `be`; assess spelling separately from correct spoken tense use.

## Unit 17 · Lessons 33–34 · A fine day / What are they doing?

**Can-do:** describe a scene with several people and their current activities.

**Language:** `They're ...-ing.` `What are they doing?` `Who is ...?` Combine familiar location phrases and singular/plural continuous forms.

**A:** listen for who is doing what in the scene. Use a people/action matching grid with text initially hidden. Reconstruct the passage from the picture using short sentences.

**B:** play a live scene reporter: the learner describes a new picture while the teacher places people and actions on a simple sketch. Ask follow-up questions and reverse roles.

**Sound / literacy:** distinguish `he's/she's` from `they're`; keep action-word stress clear. Read four new captions and identify a mismatch. Write five or six scene sentences. Begin **prepared dictation** using two or three already-corrected sentences from Lessons 31–32: hear the whole sentence, hear meaningful chunks, hear it again, then check. Use [L33](../lesson-033.html) and [L34](../lesson-034.html).

**Exit / recycle:** describe four simultaneous actions and ask two accurate questions. Recheck pronouns, `is/are`, and the earlier `-ing` spelling targets.

## Unit 18 · Lessons 35–36 · Our village / Where ...?

**Can-do:** describe a simple place and explain position or movement through it.

**Language:** source phrases with `in`, `on`, `near`, `under`, `over`, `into`, `out of`, `across`, `along`, and `between`, as illustrated. Combine these with known actions; location and movement answer different questions.

**A:** listen to the village description and trace the described locations on the picture. Demonstrate a moving token to distinguish `in` from `into` and `out of`.

**B:** describe a route taken by a person, animal, or vehicle on a simple scene. The listener moves a token; swap roles. This is movement-description practice, not a requirement to learn a full set of new street-direction commands.

**Sound / literacy:** practise phrase boundaries in `going / across the bridge`; avoid a pause between a preposition and its object. Read captions and draw arrows. Write five scene or movement sentences; dictate prepared sentences from Unit 17. Use [L35](../lesson-035.html) and [L36](../lesson-036.html).

**Exit / recycle:** describe three locations and three movements correctly, using the picture to disambiguate. Mix room descriptions and continuous actions in review.

## Unit 19 · Lessons 37–38 · Making a bookcase / What are you going to do?

**Can-do:** distinguish what is happening now from an intention.

**Language:** `What are you doing?` `I'm making ...` `What are you going to do?` `I'm going to ...` `paint it ...` Use `be going to + base verb` for the planned action.

**A:** listen for what the person is making and the intended colour. Sort two picture moments into “now” and “plan”, then ask the corresponding questions.

**B:** plan a simple room or craft task: current action, next action, and intended result. Both partners ask about plans. Include explicit owner cards when practising `your` → `my/our`.

**Sound / literacy:** hear the difference between `I'm painting` and `I'm going to paint`; practise unstressed `to` after the full form is clear. Read three now/plan captions and match them to moments. Write three paired descriptions; dictate prepared Unit 18 sentences. Use [L37](../lesson-037.html) and [L38](../lesson-038.html).

**Exit / recycle:** give three current actions and three intentions with appropriate forms. Review the ownership error from the earlier report without claiming that a speaker change always changes the owner.

## Unit 20 · Lessons 39–40 · Don't drop it! / What are you going to do?

**Can-do:** explain an intention involving an object, offer an item in the textbook pattern, and give a warning.

**Language:** `I'm going to ...` `I'm going to give ... to ...` `Don't ...` and familiar `me/him/her/us/them`. Use the object's actual referent to choose `it/them`.

**A:** listen for the intended action and why a warning is given. Act out carrying an empty box or use pictures; model the difference between a positive instruction and a prohibition.

**B:** organise a fictional delivery: say what will go to whom, ask about another person's plan, and warn about one pictured mistake. The listener uses the description to assign the object to the correct recipient.

**Sound / literacy:** make `don't` audible; practise natural grouping in `give it / to her`. Read a short plan and identify the recipient of each object. Write four intention statements and two warnings; dictate prepared Unit 19 sentences. Use [L39](../lesson-039.html) and [L40](../lesson-040.html).

**Exit / recycle:** complete the Lessons 1–40 mission, including a now/plan contrast and an object-pronoun choice. Keep the new task understandable without introducing unrelated future forms.

## Unit 21 · Lessons 41–42 · Penny's bag / Is there a ...? Is there any ...?

**Can-do:** say what a bag contains and express simple quantities.

**Language:** `There is a ...` `There is some ...` `Is there any ...?` `a piece of`, `a loaf of`, `a bar of`, `a bottle of`, and other source measure phrases. Distinguish the counted container/unit from its contents.

**A:** listen and match the bag's contents to quantity pictures. Handle a bottle, a packet, or picture cards to make the quantities concrete.

**B:** pack a picnic bag from a checklist. One partner knows the contents; the other asks questions and identifies what is missing. Model a question before using a new quantity phrase.

**Sound / literacy:** practise the weak `of` in a clear phrase such as `a bottle of milk`. Read an inventory and compare it with a picture. Write a bag inventory and three questions; dictate prepared Unit 20 sentences. Use [L41](../lesson-041.html) and [L42](../lesson-042.html).

**Exit / recycle:** distinguish `some bread`, `a piece of bread`, and `two pieces of bread` in a new packing task. Recheck the historical `some piece` error. Recognise dated product vocabulary without requiring personally irrelevant purchases.

## Unit 22 · Lessons 43–44 · Hurry up! / Are there any ...? Is there any ...?

**Can-do:** find ingredients or supplies and report what is available or missing.

**Language:** `Is there any ...?` `Are there any ...?` `There isn't any ...` `There aren't any ...` `There is/are some ...` Mix mass nouns and plural count nouns.

**A:** listen for the supplies needed and where they are. Use the cupboard picture to ask existence and location questions separately.

**B:** make tea using a pretend kitchen inventory. The learner must ask about both ingredients and objects, identify a missing item, and state an appropriate next action with `going to`.

**Sound / literacy:** hear the difference between singular and plural question openings; make negative forms clear. Read two cupboard lists and identify shortages. Write a short stock report with positive and negative sentences; dictate prepared Unit 21 sentences. Use [L43](../lesson-043.html) and [L44](../lesson-044.html).

**Exit / recycle:** ask and answer six mixed inventory questions, with at least one location follow-up. Teach `some` in positive statements and `any` in neutral questions/negatives as a starting pattern, not an absolute rule: offers and some requests can use `some`.

## Unit 23 · Lessons 45–46 · The boss's letter / Can you ...?

**Can-do:** ask about ability, state a limitation, and make a polite request.

**Language:** `Can you ...?` `Yes, I can.` `No, I can't.` `I can ... but I can't ...` `Can you ..., please?` Use the base verb after `can`, without `to`.

**A:** listen for who can type and what the problem with the letter is. Ask questions that distinguish the person's ability from a particular difficulty.

**B:** assign jobs for a small office or event. Each person has a skills card; the learner asks about abilities, chooses a helper, and makes a request. Treat `Can you open the window, please?` as a request in context, not merely a test of physical ability.

**Sound / literacy:** compare unstressed `can` in a positive statement with stressed negative `can't`. Use the recording's accent consistently and do not rely on hearing a strong final `/t/` alone. Read skills cards and assign jobs. Write an ability profile and two requests; dictate prepared Unit 22 sentences. Use [L45](../lesson-045.html) and [L46](../lesson-046.html).

**Exit / recycle:** start with a fresh task because earlier writing was fully correct. If successful, shorten form drills and concentrate on spontaneous questions, comprehension, and polite delivery.

## Unit 24 · Lessons 47–48 · A cup of coffee / Do you like ...? Do you want ...?

**Can-do:** offer refreshments, express preferences, and distinguish general liking from wanting something now.

**Language:** `Do you like ...?` `Do you want ...?` `Yes, I do.` `No, I don't.` `I like ...` `I don't want ...` `Yes, please.` `No, thank you.` Contrast these questions with `Are you ...?` and `Can you ...?`.

**A:** listen for the guest's drink and whether milk or sugar is wanted. Ask preference and immediate-choice questions so an answer cannot be chosen from grammar alone.

**B:** host a visitor using a small menu and inventory. Swap host/guest roles; change one preference and make one item unavailable. The learner offers an alternative and confirms the final choice.

**Sound / literacy:** hear `Do you ...?` as a phrase; keep `don't` and the contrasting choice clear. Read an order note and detect a wrong item. Write a 6–8 line café exchange or short hosting note; dictate prepared Unit 23 sentences. Use [L47](../lesson-047.html) and [L48](../lesson-048.html).

**Exit / recycle:** complete the final integrated mission in the main plan and repeat a shorter version after seven days. Existing perfect written work supports moving quickly, but listening and interaction still need their own evidence. Third-person `does` and later-course tense systems are not required by this unit's core task.
