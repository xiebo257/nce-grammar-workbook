# NCE 1, Lessons 1–48: learn to use the English

This is a teaching plan for our one-to-one lessons. The goal is to understand people, respond, ask your own questions, and complete simple everyday tasks using the language in Lessons 1–48. Vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, listening, speaking, reading, and writing all serve that goal.

The plan is based on your 144 supplied photographs and the teaching guidance in [the preface](../preface.html). [The source map](source-map.md) identifies the photographs for every lesson. The existing `lesson-001.html`–`lesson-048.html` pages are supplementary grammar workbooks, rather than the main dialogue texts.

Start with the diagnostic below, follow the [24 unit plans](units-001-024.md), and use the [complete Lessons 1–2 teaching script](lesson-001-002.md) as the model for our sessions. **Oral practice and correction are required:** follow the [speaking tests and correction procedure](oral-practice-and-tests.md) for every unit.

## What “mastered” will mean

For a particular unit, you can:

- Understand its main message and key details when you hear familiar language in a changed situation.
- Answer questions and ask your own without reading a model.
- Use the patterns with different people, objects, and intentions.
- Speak clearly enough to be understood; a particular native accent is not required.
- Read a short related text and write an appropriate response.
- Do these things again after a delay.

Completing a page, recognising a rule, or reciting one memorised conversation provides useful evidence, but does not establish all these abilities. Lessons 1–48 cover part of elementary English; completing them cannot establish mastery of the whole language.

### First meeting: 15–20 minute diagnostic

This samples language across the whole requested range because your written history already reaches Lesson 48. An unfamiliar item is not a failure; it tells us where to begin.

1. **Listening, 3 minutes:** I read two new short dialogues using the course language, with the text hidden. You identify the object or person and answer four simple questions. You may ask for a repeat.
2. **Interaction, 5 minutes:** return an object to its owner, introduce someone, and offer a drink. I change one detail in each exchange. You also ask me three questions.
3. **Description, 3 minutes:** describe three things in your room and one action happening now. Then state one simple intention if you can.
4. **Reading and writing, 4 minutes:** read a new six-line dialogue and write a four-line reply or continuation. Include one ownership change and one plural.
5. **Feedback, 2–5 minutes:** choose the first unit that needs teaching, and schedule quick checks for earlier units.

If we have only text chat, steps 1 and oral pronunciation assessment stay **not assessed**. Typed dialogue can establish language choice and written accuracy, but it cannot establish listening or spoken fluency. Use a voice-capable session, an available course recording, or a human reader for actual listening. The supplied ZIP contains photographs, not recordings; no audio has been created or assessed with this plan.

## Schedule and lesson rhythm

Default schedule: **24 teaching units, two units per week, approximately 12 teaching weeks**. Each unit consists of two 60-minute meetings, corresponding to the odd and even lessons. Add 10–15 minutes of home practice on five days each week. This matches the preface's basic pace; review or reteaching can extend it. If you have only 30 minutes at a time, split each meeting across two days.

| Meeting A: understand and use the odd lesson | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| Recall older language without notes | 5 |
| Nine-step dialogue presentation, including listening and reading aloud | 20 |
| Answer questions and ask your own | 10 |
| Practise the new pattern with varied cues | 10 |
| Complete a changed situation or role-play | 10 |
| Exit check and select home practice | 5 |

| Meeting B: extend with the even lesson | Minutes |
| --- | ---: |
| Recall the dialogue and previous targets | 5 |
| Hear and distinguish the target forms | 5 |
| Repetition and substitution with picture or object cues | 15 |
| Information gap or practical mission; swap roles | 15 |
| Read, then complete selected written work | 10 |
| Fresh exit task and arrange review | 10 |

The interaction and exit blocks in Meeting B include an oral test and initial correction. Use the next meeting's retrieval block for a fresh spoken attempt. Submit actual audio for pronunciation and fluency feedback; the [oral assessment guide](oral-practice-and-tests.md) specifies the recording tasks, correction report, and retest.

In a 45-minute meeting, use a 15-minute presentation, fewer successful drill items, and shorter written work. Keep the learner's own questions and the practical task. Finish remaining consolidation at home. We will aim for you to do most of the talking during practice.

### How I will teach and correct you

1. I show the situation and give one clear model.
2. We complete two examples with support.
3. You respond to four to six changed cues.
4. You use the same language to find information or make something happen.
5. I remove the model and revisit the skill later.

During a focused drill, I give a brief prompt so you can repair the target form: “Two people: **is** or **are**?” If you are unsure, I model it, you try again, and I give a new cue. During a conversation, I normally let you finish, note one or two useful corrections, and then repeat the task with new details. If meaning breaks down, we repair it immediately.

I will separate a structural error from a spelling slip, a pronunciation problem, and an acceptable alternative. I will not mark an answer wrong simply because it differs from a model. After correction, you produce a fresh example; copying the correction is only the first step.

### Pronunciation and vocabulary throughout

- Learn nouns with useful frames: `a book`, `two books`, `your book`, `on the table`.
- Learn verbs in phrases: `open the window`, `make tea`, `look at the picture`.
- Begin with a manageable active set, usually 6–10 items, then cycle through **all** the unit's new items over subsequent practice. Difficult or dated items can remain recognition vocabulary when they are not personally useful.
- Include structural words in retrieval: pronouns, articles, auxiliaries, prepositions, `one/ones`, `some/any`.
- Train listening discrimination, mouth position where helpful, word stress, sentence stress, contractions, and phrase linking in short doses.
- Follow the available recording consistently. British and American variants are acceptable when intelligible; do not treat accent variation as an error. The target is communication, not accent imitation.
- Interpret intonation in context. Rising yes/no questions and falling statements are useful starting models, not universal rules.
- Explain dated expressions briefly. For example, recognise `How do you do?` as formal and learn `Nice to meet you` as an optional modern alternative. Keep extensions separate from the required unit patterns.

The photographed even lessons contain number prompts beside the pictures. Use three minutes of each extension drill for these, rather than ignoring them as picture labels. Say a number and select its picture; reverse the direction; write a few heard numbers; then use the familiar picture for the main sentence pattern. Record number errors separately from grammatical errors.

Practise spelling in context: capitals for `I`, names, and nationalities; apostrophes in contractions and ownership; question marks; plural endings; and `-ing` spellings when they arise. Introduce letter names briefly if needed to spell a name or repair a word, labelling this as a useful addition rather than a photographed lesson objective. Dates and telling the time appear in the preface as examples of long-term skills; they are not invented core requirements for Lessons 1–48.

## Review and evidence of mastery

Review a target on the next day, then approximately 3, 7, 14, and 30 days after first learning it. Each review takes 3–8 minutes: retrieve first, check second, repeat once with a changed cue. Missed reviews resume at the next available session; there is no need to restart the course.

Suggested weekly pattern: Monday Meeting A, Tuesday Meeting B, Wednesday short review, Thursday Meeting A, Friday Meeting B, Saturday brief cumulative retrieval, Sunday optional rest or listening. Home practice totals about 10–15 minutes: 3 minutes listening, 3 minutes oral retrieval, 3 minutes questions or a role-play, and 3–6 minutes reading/writing or error repair.

For each unit, gather evidence from these checks:

| Skill | Working criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Listening | At least 4 of 5 meaning questions correct after no more than two presentations, using taught language with changed details. |
| Pattern use | At least 8 of 10 fresh mixed cues correct without seeing a model. Both affirmative and negative forms appear when taught. |
| Interaction | Complete a 6–8 turn exchange, with both roles and at least two learner-initiated questions. |
| Intelligibility | The listener can identify the intended words and message; practise any recurring sound that blocks this. |
| Reading | At least 4 of 5 meaning or detail questions correct on a short new text within scope. |
| Writing | At least 4 of 5 target uses correct, plus an understandable short original response. |
| Retention | Repeat a shortened mixed check after 7 days without notes. |

These are practical decision rules for our lessons, not standardised proficiency scores. Keep skills separate: a high writing score cannot compensate for listening that has not been assessed. A request such as `Pardon?` is a communication strategy, not automatically an error.

Use **learning**, **independent today**, **retained after review**, or **not assessed** for each skill. If one skill needs work, give it a short repair session while continuing language you can handle. Persistent difficulty with a prerequisite, such as `is/are` before continuous forms, calls for targeted reteaching before adding more complexity.

### Cumulative missions

| After lessons | Mission | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Meet someone and return a lost object. | Identify the owner; introduce name, nationality, and job; ask and answer questions. |
| 16 | Sort clothes and luggage for a small group. | Identify ownership, colour, and singular/plural forms; repair one deliberate mix-up. |
| 24 | Welcome visitors and distribute supplies. | Introduce people, respond to needs, choose the right item, and follow requests. |
| 32 | Describe and organise a room. | Say what is there and where it is; give instructions; describe what a person is doing. |
| 40 | Explain a scene and arrange a small task. | Distinguish current action from intention; describe movement; choose and give objects. |
| 48 | Host a visitor and organise refreshments. | Combine introductions, location, quantities, ability, preferences, and requests. |

For the final mission, use an unseen room or café scenario with familiar language: receive the visitor, locate two items, ask about preferences, offer or request quantities, explain what someone can do, and state what you are going to do. Include an 8–12 turn conversation and a 6–8 sentence follow-up note. Repeat a shorter version a week later. Keep any unfamiliar task instructions outside the scored English.
