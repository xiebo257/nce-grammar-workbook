## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Past tense vs past participle | `saw` (past) / `seen` (participle); `did` / `done`; `ate` / `eaten` | Past tense: used in simple past (`I saw`). Past participle: used in present perfect (`I have seen`). Regular verbs: same form for both (`washed/washed`). |
| `Have you [verb-ed] it yet?` + `No, I haven't. I'm still [verb-ing] it.` | `Have you done it yet? No, I haven't. I'm still doing it.` | `yet` = up to now (present perfect question). `still` = continuing (present continuous). |
| `has already stopped` / `is still V-ing` | `John has already stopped, but Mary is still working.` | Present perfect (finished) + present continuous (continuing). |
| Present perfect + past simple dialogue | `Has he bought a watch? Yes, he has. When did he buy it? He bought it last week.` | Present perfect for experience → past simple for specific time. |
| Prepositional verbs: `look at + object` | `looked at them` (not `looked them`) | `at` is a preposition — it must appear before the object: "looked **at** them," not "looked them." |
