## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `Yes, [pronoun] does. [Pronoun] wants [measure] of [noun].` | `He wants two pounds of beef.` | Measure phrase = `[quantity] + of + noun`. Uncountable nouns (beef, bread, wine, tobacco) stay singular after `of`. Countable plural nouns (grapes, tomatoes) stay plural after `of`. |
| `No, [pronoun] doesn't. [Pronoun] doesn't like [noun].` | `She doesn't like lamb.` | Negate + dislike. The noun from the question is preserved in the answer. |
| `Does [pronoun] like [item1]? What about [item2]?` | `Does he like chicken? What about beef?` | Reverse-engineer questions from given answers. First question asks about item1; second uses `What about + item2?`. |
| `[Subject] wants [item1], and [pronoun] wants [item2], too.` | `He wants some oranges, and he wants some peaches, too.` | Two wants joined by `and`, ending with `, too`. The `, too` marks the second item as an addition. |
| Countable vs uncountable after `of` | `a pound of beef` (uncountable, singular) vs `a pound of grapes` (countable, plural) | Uncountable nouns stay singular after `of`. Countable nouns that are plural in the question stay plural in the answer. |
| Measure phrase pluralization | `loaves` (plural of `loaf`), `tins`, `pounds`, `pieces`, `bars`, `slices`, `bottles` | The measure noun takes the plural: `loaf`→`loaves`, `tin`→`tins`, `pound`→`pounds`, `piece`→`pieces`, `bar`→`bars`, `slice`→`slices`, `bottle`→`bottles`. The substance noun stays singular (bread, cheese, chocolate, steak, milk). |

## Key takeaway

- **Q5, Q6 — countable plural nouns after `of`.** When the question asks about a plural countable noun ("Does Ann like grapes?" / "Does your daughter like tomatoes?"), the measure phrase keeps the plural: "a pound of **grapes**" / "a pound of **tomatoes**". Uncountable nouns (beef, bread, wine, tobacco, mince, lamb, butter, coffee) stay singular after `of`. **Rule: the noun's number in the question is preserved in the answer — don't singularize a plural countable noun.**
- **Q12 — spelling `beeans` → `beans`.** The word has one `e`: b-e-a-n-s. Extra letters in common words are a recurring slip — slow down on high-frequency vocabulary.
- **Q22–Q28 — missing `, too`.** The Section III pattern includes `, too` at the end: "He wants some oranges, and he wants some peaches**,** **too**." The learner correctly forms the `and` conjunction and pronoun matching but omits `, too` in all 7 items. **The `, too` signals "in addition" — it's part of the pattern, not optional decoration.**
