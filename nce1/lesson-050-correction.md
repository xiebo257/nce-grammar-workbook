## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `[S1], but [S2].` | `He likes butter, but he doesn't like cheese.` | Join two related sentences with `, but `. Keep the subject pronoun in the second clause. |
| `Do/Does [subject] like [item1] or [item2]?` | `Does he like jam or honey?` | `or` question offering two choices. `Do` for `you`/`they`; `Does` for `he`/`she`/`it`/[name]. |
| `[Pronoun] likes [item1], but [other] doesn't.` | `He likes jam, but his brother doesn't.` | State what the subject likes, then contrast with `but [other] doesn't`. |
| `[Pronoun] likes [item2].` | `He likes honey.` | The other person likes the alternative item. Pronoun matches the other person. |
| Third-person singular `-s` | `she likes`, `he likes`, `Sam likes` | Add `-s` to the verb for `he`/`she`/`it`/[name] in present simple: `like` → `likes`. |
