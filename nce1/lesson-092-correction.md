## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `am/is/are going to` -> `will` | `Are they going to have a party?` -> `Yes, they'll have a party.` | Replace `am/is/are going to` with `will`; keep the main verb in its base form. |
| Future contraction with a third-person subject | `Is she going to have a holiday?` -> `Yes, she'll have a holiday.` | `he'll`/`she'll`/`it'll` = `he/she/it will`; plural `they'll` = `they will`. |
| Future contraction with `I` | `Are you going to telephone her?` -> `Yes, I'll telephone her.` | In the answer, question `you` usually becomes respondent `I`: `I'll` = `I will`. |
| Possessive viewpoint shift | `your table and chairs` -> `my table and chairs` | When the question addresses `you` and the answer is from the speaker's viewpoint, change `your` to `my`. |
| Full affirmative reply | `Yes, it will snow next month.` | For Section I, include `Yes,` before the future statement; Q9's omission is a non-scoring pattern note. |
| Short affirmative answer | `Will he paint...?` -> `Yes, he will.` | Echo `will` and the subject pronoun: `he`, `she`, `they`, or `I`. |
| Short negative answer | `Will they be...?` -> `No, they won't.` | Use `won't` = `will not` and match the question's subject. |
| Short-answer viewpoint shift | `Will you finish...?` -> `No, we won't.` / `No, I won't.` | `you` becomes `I` or `we` according to the intended respondent. |
| Sentence punctuation | `No, she won't.` | Finish a complete short answer with a period; Q23 is correct apart from this final mark. |
