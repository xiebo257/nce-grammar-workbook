## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `could ... but couldn't ...` | `He could read, but he couldn't write.` | Split a two-part ability question into one positive and one negative clause. |
| No article for sports | `play tennis`, `play football` | Sports names do not take `the`; `play the tennis` is incorrect. |
| `can` → `could` for past ability | `He can run fast now.` → `He could run fast ten years ago, too.` | Shift `can` to `could` and add the past time phrase; `too` conveys "as well as now". |
| `too + adj + to + verb` (past) | `It was too hard to eat.` | The subject is the thing being acted upon; verb is in the infinitive. |
| `too + adj + for + pronoun + to + verb` (present) | `It's too high for him to reach.` | Add `for + pronoun` to specify who is affected. |
| `not + adj + enough + to + verb` | `He wasn't old enough to go to school.` | The negative of "enough" expresses why something couldn't be done. |
| Possessive ellipsis | `his sister's` | The omitted noun (homework) is recoverable from context. |
