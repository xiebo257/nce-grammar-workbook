## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| `have to` — present | `I have to` / `he has to` | Third-person singular takes `has to`, not `have to`. |
| `have to` — future | `I shall have to` / `you will have to` | `shall` with first person, `will` with second/third. |
| `have to` — past | `I had to` / `you had to` | No auxiliary; `had to` is invariable across persons. |
| `don't have to` — present negative | `I don't have to` / `he doesn't have to` | Use `do/does` + `not` + `have to`. |
| `won't have to` — future negative | `I won't have to do it tomorrow, either` | `will not` → `won't`; use `either` in negative extensions. |
| `must` vs `don't need to` | `You must wash your hands but you don't need to wash your face` | `must` expresses necessity; `don't need to` expresses lack of necessity. |
| `some` → `any` | `he doesn't have to buy any today` | Negative polarity replaces `some` with `any`. |
| Phrasal verb + pronoun | `hand them in` (not `hand in them`) | Pronoun objects go before the particle; noun objects may go either side. |
| Viewpoint shift | `Do I have to…?` → `You have to…` | The responder addresses the questioner as `you`; echoing the question's pronoun is also valid. |

## Key takeaway

- Third-person singular present takes `has to`, not `have to`.
- With a phrasal verb like `hand in`, a pronoun object must precede the particle: `hand them in`.
- `must` asserts necessity; `don't need to` denies necessity — use both to split a paired question.
- Pronoun substitution (`the doctor` → `him`, `lunch` → `it`) and viewpoint choice (`you` vs `we`) are free variations that do not affect correctness.
