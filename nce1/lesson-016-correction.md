## Usage table

| Pattern | Example from this lesson | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Negative answer with plural subject | `No, they aren't. They're German.` | Use `they` and `are` for plural people or things. |
| Reply for `you two` | `We aren't Dutch. We're Danish.` | When the speaker answers for two people including themself, use `we`. |
| Alternative answers | `They aren't black. They're brown.` | Reject the other option, then state the selected one. |
| Regular plural nouns | `shirt -> shirts`, `blouse -> blouses` | Add `-s` to regular nouns. |
| Irregular plural nouns | `man -> men`, `woman -> women` | Learn irregular forms rather than adding `-s`. |
| Plural `be` agreement | `Are these your passports?` | Use `are` with `these`, `those`, `we`, `you`, `they`, and plural nouns. |
| Singular-to-plural demonstratives | `This is my son. -> These are my sons.` | Change `this` to `these` and singular nouns to plural forms. |
| `Whose are ...?` | `Whose are these books?` | Match `are` to the plural thing whose owner is being asked about. |
| Adjectives after plural nouns | `My friends are Norwegian.` | Nationality and colour adjectives do not take plural `-s`. |

## Key takeaway

- Keep the sentence type when rewriting: a question must remain a question (`Is he ...?` -> `Are they ...?`).
- Check spelling carefully, especially `yellow` and `brown`.
- Use `are` with plural subjects, including `these books`, `these cars`, `your brothers`, and `they`.
- Use `we` when replying for `you two`; use `they` for other plural people or things.
- In `Whose are these books?`, the verb agrees with plural `books`.
