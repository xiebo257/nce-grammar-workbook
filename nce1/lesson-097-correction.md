## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Practical reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Singular ownership reply | `Whose book is this?` -> `It's mine.` | Use `it is`/`it's` for one thing or an uncountable noun. |
| Plural ownership reply | `Whose shoes are these?` -> `They're theirs.` | Use `they are`/`they're` for plural things; do not use singular `it's`. |
| Possessive adjective | `my pen`, `his umbrella`, `their school` | A possessive adjective comes before a noun: `my`, `your`, `his`, `her`, `our`, `their`. |
| Possessive pronoun | `mine`, `yours`, `his`, `hers`, `ours`, `theirs` | A possessive pronoun stands alone and replaces the noun phrase; it has no apostrophe. |
| Singular `belong` agreement | `This coat is hers. It belongs to her.` | One object takes `it belongs`; the owner appears as an object pronoun after `to`. |
| Plural `belong` agreement | `These toys are theirs. They belong to them.` | Multiple objects take `they belong`, without `-s` on `belong`. |
| One object with plural owners | `This ball is theirs. It belongs to them.` | Verb agreement follows the owned object (`ball`), not the number of owners. |
| Regular plural possessive | `boys' ball`, `girls' cat` | For a regular plural ending in `s`, place the apostrophe after `s`. |
| Irregular plural possessive | `men's shoes`, `women's handbags`, `children's toys` | Irregular plurals do not end in `s`, so add `'s`. |
