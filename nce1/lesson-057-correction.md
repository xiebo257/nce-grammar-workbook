## Usage table

| Pattern | Example | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Present simple (habitual) | `John often plays football` / `They often speak English` | Singular subject → verb + `-s`/`-es`. Plural subject → base form. Adverb (`often`/`usually`) before the verb. |
| Present continuous (temporary) | `he is playing basketball now` / `they are swimming today` | `is/are + verb-ing`. Used for actions happening now/today/at the moment. |
| Contrast: `but` | `John often plays football, but he is playing basketball now.` | Join habitual and temporary with `, but `. The pronoun in the second clause matches the subject. |
| CVC doubling in `-ing` | `sit` → `sitting`, `swim` → `swimming` | One-syllable CVC verbs double the final consonant before `-ing`. |
| `-e` dropping in `-ing` | `shave` → `shaving`, `come` → `coming` | Verbs ending in silent `-e` drop the `e` before `-ing`. |
| Section II four-line frame | `No, she isn't. She's sleeping. No, she doesn't. She usually sleeps at night.` | (1) Negate present continuous + state what IS happening. (2) Negate present simple + state the usual habit. |
| `isn't`/`aren't` (present continuous negation) | `No, she isn't.` / `No, they aren't.` | Singular → `isn't`; plural → `aren't`. |
| `doesn't`/`don't` (present simple negation) | `No, she doesn't.` / `No, they don't.` | Singular → `doesn't`; plural → `don't`. After `doesn't`/`don't`, verb is base form. |
